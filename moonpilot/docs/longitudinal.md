# The longitudinal strategy
`moonpilot/longitudinal.py` is the fork's own planner — what acceleration to ask for — and `moonpilot/longcontrol.py` its own acceleration controller, how that acceleration is tracked. Two seams pick them: `moonpilot_longitudinal_planner()` in plannerd, `moonpilot_longcontrol()` in controlsd, both factory calls returning `None` when the toggle is off, so upstream's acados MPC and upstream's PI loop stay on the line as the fallback. The things about them that are not obvious:

- **The cruise candidate is the driver's own ladder, interpolated, not a gain.** `cruise_accel` is `np.interp` of the speed error over `MOONPILOT_CRUISE_ERR_BP` (−5, −3, −1.5, 0, 2, 5 m/s) onto medium brake, light brake, coasting, hold, slow accel and fast accel — each rung measured from ~4 h of this driver's manual longitudinal on the RAV4 (308 segments, grade removed against each segment's median pitch): slow accel is the median of accelerating gas episodes' mean per start-speed band (0.77…0.31 m/s²), fast accel the p75 of *instantaneous* accel at each speed while the episode is still ≥ 5 m/s short of where it ends — the ladder's own fast-rung condition — so it carries a launch's shape rather than its average (0.83 at 0.5 m/s, ~1.97 at 2.5–3.5, 1.27 at 11, 0.87 at 17.5; `cruise_cap`'s 1.7 combined budget still bounds the peak): flown from rest the planner reads 0.83 / 1.28 / 1.70 / 1.45 / 0.87 m/s² at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 6 / 8 s and 11.3 m/s at 8 s, against this driver's 0.78 / 1.40 / 2.20 / 0.59 / 0.34 and 12.1 m/s, coasting the no-pedal level-road median (−0.10…−0.33), light/medium brake the p90/median of 316 brake episodes' mean decel (−0.75/−1.25). Hard brake (p10, −1.75) is measured and deliberately not a cruise rung. `MOONPILOT_FAST_ACCEL_V` is also `cruise_cap`'s comfort curve, so one table bounds every positive ask. `forceDecel` (`v_cruise` 0) keeps the pre-ladder law floored at medium brake, because the coasting rung would soften driver-monitoring escalation at low speed. Below creep speed (`MOONPILOT_CREEP_SPEED`, 1.9 m/s, where a no-pedal car settles) `crawl_accel` caps the spacing regulator's positive ask at fast crawl (+0.31) unless the lead accelerates at least that hard, fading out by twice creep speed. The two braking crawl rungs — slow crawl −0.08, stopped −0.47 over the last second into a stop (p25 −0.81, p75 −0.26) — are measured and **not** applied, because the unshaped regulator already stops at that rate: −0.31 over the last second and −0.47 over the two before, resting 5.19 m back from every approach speed 5–25 m/s. Compressing its braking toward the rungs softened the finish further (−0.28) and rested 4.63 m back. The corpus tops out at 25 m/s; above it the last rung holds.
- **The personality dial moves the ladder's error axis, never its rung magnitudes.** `MOONPILOT_ERR_BP_SCALE` — aggressive 0.7, standard 1.0, relaxed 1.4 — scales `MOONPILOT_CRUISE_ERR_BP` per frame's `selfdriveState.personality` (raw-value keyed, like `MOONPILOT_T_FOLLOW`): aggressive lands the driver's measured medium brake at −3.5 m/s of speed error instead of −5, relaxed needs −7, and the hold entry stays at 0 so the ladder keeps its shape — the same measured rungs binding earlier or later. The factors are hand-picked because the corpus cannot derive them: during manual driving `carState.vCruise` is valid on only ~5 % of frames (the stock ACC is off), so the driver's own error-to-rung response was never recorded. Nothing safety-side reads the axis — the lead's regulator, TTC and stopping floor, `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL`, `cruise_cap`, the jerk limit — and `forceDecel` short-circuits at `v_cruise` 0 before the interpolation. A re-measure over the corpus as it stands (265 of the original 308 segments survive on disk) corroborates the rungs rather than re-adopting anything: brake within 0.19 and coasting within 0.05 m/s² of the published values, the accel bands keeping their sign and order with the high-band deltas sitting where those missing segments' attrition is worst.
- **The vehicle's positive comfort jerk can tighten onroad, but never loosen safety braking.** `moonpilot/jerk.py` pairs the existing lag-aligned `a_target` command slope with `carState.aEgo` only while the fork owns longitudinal control, then slowly filters a ratio after 100 valid positive ramps. `MoonpilotLongJerkScale` persists the applied scale, defaults to 1.0, and is clamped to 0.5…1.0; `jerk_limit` applies it only to the positive comfort/launch ramp. The stopping-tested down ramp and `MOONPILOT_JERK_EMERGENCY` are untouched, and no estimate is exactly the nominal planner.

- **Cruise acceleration uses the runway before a slower lead.** For each present lead below set speed, the policy reserves the larger of the standstill gap and the requested headway, then estimates the relative distance needed to reach set speed at the current cruise ask and match the lead at `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL`. Below that distance, the cruise ask is capped at the measured slow accel rung; over one extra follow gap it blends back to the ordinary ladder. Current closing speed is part of that distance, so a slower lead tightens the cap even at the same range. This changes only positive cruise acceleration, and a clear road still uses the ordinary personality-scaled ladder. A lead accelerating by at least the existing fast-crawl threshold bypasses the cap; otherwise the estimate holds lead speed constant until the next planner tick.

- **The policy is closed-form, with one approach for every slower lead.** Four lead terms compete by `min`: the spacing regulator, relative-speed arrival, TTC and the stopping floor. The regulator holds `gap == max(STOP_DISTANCE, t_follow * v_ego)` with its braking capped at `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL` (1.0 m/s²); the standstill distance is a floor under headway, not an offset. Arrival targets `max(MOONPILOT_STOP_REST, t_follow * v_lead_eff)`, continuously from a stopped lead to a moving one. TTC and the stopping floor retain their independent admission gates and can demand stronger braking. Cruise, curve, corridor and model candidates remain separate; the smallest wins, ties keeping the earlier candidate. The published trajectory rolls the same policy forward.
  On a closing approach, only the spacing regulator gets a soft inner cushion:
  `min(MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_CUSHION, max(v_ego - v_lead_match, 0) * K_V / K_GAP,
  max(t_follow * v_ego - STOP_DISTANCE, 0))`. Below its 1 m cap that moving target exactly cancels
  the relative-speed brake at the nominal gap, so mild closing may cross it instead of treating the
  target as a wall. The cushion collapses with closing
  speed; once the speeds match, the speed floor (below) holds the command there, so the gap rests where
  the speeds met rather than being bought back with speed — a 0.196 m crossing at 20 m/s rests 0.165 m
  inside a 29 m target. The third bound tapers the cushion
  out before the time-gap target reaches the standstill floor. This never reaches the TTC or
  stopping-floor terms, so every stopping bound remain
  intact.
- **The lead slot coasts in measured low-speed stop-and-go, then yields to safety.** Across six clean
  manual launch→brake cycles in the route corpus, all six ego speeds were 1.58–3.87 m/s and the driver
  coasted before braking in every case; coast occupied 85 % of the first 1.5 s after the detected lead
  deceleration, against 8 % braking and 7 % gas. The follow cushion itself is already zero below
  `STOP_DISTANCE / t_follow` (4.1 m/s at standard personality), so this cap remains separate inside
  the lead slot: the ego must be closing at at most 4.0 m/s, whether the lead is moving or stopped, with the gap
  at most 10 m, and the estimated lead acceleration at most −0.3 m/s². It takes the measured
  level-road coast value for the ego speed (−0.10 m/s² up to 3.5 m/s, easing to −0.125 at 4.0).
  **Closing is the cue, not the lead's braking alone**: a braking lead that is still pulling away
  opens the gap, and capping there braked a launching car behind it and, as the opening gap crossed
  the 10 m edge, turned into an accelerate/brake/accelerate wave. While closing, the gap only shrinks,
  so that edge is crossed inward once. `policy` applies the cap with `min` **after** `lead_accel`, so the TTC
  and stopping-floor candidates still win whenever the physical gap requires more braking. When the
  lead's estimated acceleration leaves that band, the cap disappears on the next frame and the ordinary
  spacing/cruise candidates recover follow distance; there is no stored cushion or launch state. The
  speed and gap ceilings are deliberate: the corpus has no engaged high-speed stop-and-go evidence, and
  high-speed coasts cross the approach bounds described below.
- **An oversized gap never raises the cruise slot.** At or above the set speed, the ordinary
  speed-error term is non-positive and remains the ceiling even when the spacing regulator wants to
  close a larger gap. A transient brake can therefore leave the car farther back behind a lead
  holding the set speed; recovery waits for the lead to pull away or the ego to fall below set speed
  rather than buying follow-distance recovery with extra acceleration or overspeed.
- **The model's braking ask passes through outside experimental mode, gated only by a deadband.** `MoonpilotModelBraking` (on by default) admits `modelV2.action.desiredAcceleration` as a fourth candidate in every mode, but only once it asks for at least `MOONPILOT_MODEL_BRAKE_THRESHOLD` of braking — without that deadband `min` turns model dither into a one-way ratchet against the cruise term and the car settles below the set speed on a clear road. Past it the ask goes in whole, so a deep model brake reaches full authority on `MOONPILOT_JERK_EMERGENCY`'s ramp. What bounds it is `ACCEL_MIN`, and that bound is not a fork value: the clip every command passes through in `update`, the identical clip in the published rollout, and the controller's own `accel_limits` all enforce it, and nothing bypasses them — `controlsState.forceDecel` is driver-monitoring escalation, not an emergency override. Because `policy` takes the minimum, the candidate can only ever add braking, so the fork's "at least as much braking as the kinematic law" argument is untouched. The model's `action.shouldStop` deliberately stays experimental-only: it is `v_ego < 0.3 and a < 0.1`, the creep state the should-stop trailing gate exists to exclude. A fork-owned floor *above* `ACCEL_MIN` was tried and removed: it withheld braking the driver had already begun, and its `meta.hardBrakePredicted` release valve never fired.
- **A stop the model began is a stop the model finishes.** The deadband is hysteresis once the model holds the slot: it keeps it until its ask rises past `model_release(v_ego)`, which is the entry deadband at and above `MOONPILOT_MODEL_STOP_SPEED` (5 m/s) and climbs to `should_stop`'s own 0.1 at creep speed. On route `000003d2` the model braked a clear-road stop from 17 m/s and its tapering ask crossed back over −0.5 at 0.9 m/s; the cruise term took the slot, asked +1.0, and was moving the car again when the driver braked. In the crawl band the rule is now exactly experimental mode's stop contract — held while the model's ask is under 0.1 — so the car also launches when the model asks to go. That is the ceiling: the model, not a lead, decides when a lead-less stop ends. Over that route's eight lead-less standstills the ask crossed 0.1 between 0.3 and 5.4 s before the driver moved off on six and not at all on two (the car would have held), and on two long parked waits (73 s, 12 min) it crossed a minute or more early — the driver is the backstop there, as in experimental mode. At speed nothing changed, so there is still no ratchet below the set speed.
- **Stopped and moving leads share relative-speed arrival.** `lead_accel` evaluates `arrival_accel(closing, gap - target, a_lead)` whenever braking-previewed closing is positive, with `target = max(MOONPILOT_STOP_REST, t_follow * v_lead_eff)` and `v_lead_eff = max(0, v_lead + min(a_lead, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T)`. It spends the available closing distance rather than holding set speed until the spacing regulator finally asks to brake. There is no separate stopped-lead roll-in, nor a gate at 0.1 m/s or `STOP_DISTANCE / t_follow`: a lead slowing to rest changes the same target continuously. Positive lead acceleration is credited only to the comfort arrival and tracking terms, not to TTC or the stopping floor.
- **Catch-up fades out before stop-and-go.** Below set speed and while the lead is not braking (`a_lead > -MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_TAU_RESET`), arrival can earn the existing slow-rung credit while the gentle coast still has room to match. That credit is weighted continuously from 0 when `t_follow * v_lead_eff` equals `MOONPILOT_STOP_REST` to 1 at `MOONPILOT_STOP_DISTANCE`, where the former moving-lead catch-up began. A crawling or stopped target therefore gets no catch-up throttle, without a binary moving/stopped switch. The cruise candidate remains a ceiling and the no-throttle coast still includes grade when computing the catch-up room.
- **The rest point is a soft aim, not a final-meter precision chase.** Arrival floors its slack at `MOONPILOT_ARRIVAL_MIN_T * closing` (1.5 s), so its braking falls with closing even after the preferred 5.2 m rest point is crossed. The separate `-v_ego² / (2 * max(gap - STOP_REST, 0.1))` crawl finish is removed. At the reproduced state of 0.68 m/s and 5.2 m gap, the raw lead ask is now approximately −0.65 m/s² instead of −2.31. The spacing regulator still brakes a gap deficit; this is not permission to drive through the lead.
- **The stopping-floor bound is unchanged.** Its anchor remains `STOP_REST + min(STOP_TAPER_T * v_ego, STOP_DISTANCE - STOP_REST)`, with a 1 m minimum slack and the existing 1.0…1.6 m/s² speed-scheduled admission. Once admitted, `min(a, a_stop)` preserves at least its braking. TTC, lead-brake sustain, FCW, `ACCEL_MIN` and the emergency jerk ramp are unchanged. Upstream's maneuver suite does not cover the highest-speed stopped-lead regime; the fork retains its direct closed-loop coverage at 118.8, 129.6 and 144 kph. Lead range remains pipeline-limited at approximately 131.9 m, not the radar's 228 m sensor limit.
- **Radar latency remains part of the lead's age.** Toyota's `dRel` is older than the `carState` radard uses to compose `vLead`, even where `CP.radarDelay` is zero. `moonpilot/radar_latency.py` learns that residual from `vLead / aEgo` for stationary radar targets, requiring `|aEgo| ≥ 0.6`, a ratio in (0, 0.3] s, and 50 gated samples before applying it. `_lead_age` adds that value only for radar leads; `MoonpilotRadarLatency` and `MoonpilotRadarLatencySamples` persist the estimate and its evidence. The stopping-floor anchor still tapers from 6 m toward the 5.2 m soft rest target below 4 m/s. It is a bounded aim, not a promise to finish on an exact centimeter; closed-loop regressions retain at least the floor's one-meter slack allowance below that aim.
- **A lead that pulls away lifts the stop hold at once, from inside the standstill gap.** Every stop finishes inside the 6 m standstill gap (the rest point is 5.2 m; the car rests nearer 4.5 m on the road), so at the launch the regulator's spacing error is a brake — −0.44 m/s² from 4.5 m — and on route `000003d2` it kept the hold on for a second after the lead moved, until the driver took over with the gas. `departure_crawl` floors the regulator, while the lead is moving and faster than the ego, at its relative-speed term alone capped at `MOONPILOT_FAST_CRAWL`, fading over the crawl band like `crawl_accel`; the lead's speed carries its accel both ways, so a braking lead is owed nothing, and speed-matched the floor is zero — the gap is kept at a crawl, not recovered, and every braking term still reads the physical gap. With the car parked, a negative plan target is also no longer a jerk baseline (the hold owns the brake), so the release is two frames after the lead's speed clears the rest deadband. Replayed against that launch: the hold lifts ~0.5 s sooner, the car crawls at +0.31, and the driver's fast-accel ladder then takes it to +1.70 within 2 s.

- **The floor is measured in the frame the gap actually closes in, and it is where the fork's worst braking defect lived.** `stopping_decel(v_ego, v_lead, a_lead, slack, sustain)` replaces `-(v_ego² - v_lead²) / (2·slack)`, which is exact only for a *stopped* lead: while the lead keeps travelling the gap closes at the relative rate, so what spends the slack is `(v_ego - v_lead)² / 2a`, and the absolute form was deeper than the geometry by `(v_ego + v_lead) / (v_ego - v_lead)` for every moving one. On the device's route `000003c8` that was 0.82 m/s² deeper than the geometry on 45 of 45 engaged braking ticks (a 4.4–5.3× over-ask), and because the regulator's output is positive at those gaps and the TTC term is not yet binding, the floor was the *only* admitted braking candidate — it governed by default, and the command sat flat on it: **a −1.0…−1.17 m/s² plateau held ~8 s behind a lead 70–115 m away**, which is what a driver reports as "braking too strong too early, like a decel floor rather than a ramp". The positive surge after it was the cruise slot refilling the speed error the phantom brake dug, not a second defect: post-brake recovery peaks are unchanged by the fix (max +1.60 either way). What ships now is one continuous law in two exact pieces, verified against a brute-force minimum-constant-decel solver (1 ms integration, bisection) to ≤0.002 m/s² on states spanning both:
  - the **late crossing**, where the ego is still faster when the lead's braking ends: `-(v_ego - v_end)² / (2·(slack + 0.5·|a_lead|·t²))`, with `t = min(sustain, time to the lead's rest)` and `v_end` the speed that leaves it. The lead's travel beyond a lead permanently at `v_end` cancels analytically and leaves that `0.5·|a_lead|·t²` of extra room.
  - the **early crossing**, `2·slack <= closing · t`, where the ego matches the lead's speed while the lead is still braking. Crediting the whole braking-phase travel there credits travel the lead has not made: at `v_ego` 25 behind a lead doing 10 and braking at −0.5 with 25 m of slack the late form reads −2.50 where the truth is −5.00, because the ego comes to rest at 10 s having been given the lead's full 20 s. The relative speeds converge at `a - |a_lead|`, so the requirement is `|a_lead| + closing² / (2·slack)`. The two pieces agree exactly at the boundary, so this is a continuous law rather than a blend.

  **`MOONPILOT_LEAD_BRAKE_SUSTAIN_T` (2.0 s) is the dial, and it is sized from both ends**, flown closed-loop through the real planner (contact = gap < 0.4 m). No prediction at all (`sustain` 0) contacts a lead braking −3.5 or −5 to rest from the 1.45 s follow gap at 20 m/s, and a 15 m cut-in that then brakes at −4. Predicting the lead all the way to rest is safe but turns a lead *tapping* −5 for half a second at 30 m/s from 60 m into a −3.50 m/s² command for an end gap that is ~40 m either way — deeper than the 3.36 that capped `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` at 0.5 s, on that same scenario. At 2.0 s the tap costs −1.00 m/s² against the absolute-frame law's −1.89 (these sizing numbers are on the flat −1.0 admission; on the scheduled one the tap reads −0.43 and the −3.5 end gap 5.06), while the sustained margins hold: end gaps 5.00 m at −3.5 to rest (5.09 before), 5.70 m at −5 (5.51), 4.91 m on the cut-in (4.97), and the four pinned stopped-lead approaches bit-identical at 5.17–5.18 m. 1.5 s gives 2.88 m on the −5 case and 3.0 s puts the tap back at −1.86. A lead braking at −8 contacts at every horizon including infinity: the ego needs more than `ACCEL_MIN` there, which is physics and not this constant.

  **FCW keeps the unbounded horizon** — `required_decel` passes `sustain=inf`, so it predicts the lead to rest. The two differ deliberately: the planner's bound exists so a brake tap does not buy −3.5 m/s², and a warning behind its own −4 m/s² gate has no comfort to protect, so the only thing a shorter horizon can do there is stay silent through a real one (at `v_ego` 30 behind a lead doing 28 at 25 m braking at −6, to-rest reads −5.00 and fires where the 2 s horizon reads −2.67, and that contact is genuinely unavoidable). The relative frame reaches FCW too, and removes a false-positive class: a lead holding 28 m/s 12 m ahead of a car doing 30 reads −0.17 where the absolute form read −4.94 and fired on an ordinary overtake.

  Measured on route `000003c8` against the pre-fix law: the engaged block's plateau went to **0.00 s** with **zero new braking ticks at any threshold**, and only states with `a_lead <= -1.0` got deeper. The early-crossing piece is selected on 22 % of the planner arm's `a_lead`-live states and 29 % of FCW's.
- **A lead's acceleration is anticipated one-sidedly, and only its braking half reaches the safety terms.** `v_lead_match = v_lead + max(a_lead, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` is the speed the spacing regulator matches — the lead's *next* speed — and it never reaches the safety terms: a credit there would release braking on a predicted launch, which is braking for a prediction where the geometry had not moved. The braking direction does reach them, deliberately: `v_lead_eff = v_lead + min(a_lead, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` is the speed `closing` and the floor are measured against, so a lead that is braking is matched as if it had already shed `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` of that braking — half a second, and the length is the whole trade. It is no longer the *only* braking prediction: `stopping_decel` makes its own, `MOONPILOT_LEAD_BRAKE_SUSTAIN_T` long (and unbounded on the FCW arm), on top of this credited speed — the two stack, which is why the sustain bound had to be sized with this constant's own tap scenario in hand. (The estimator's accel reaches both terms one further way — `update` hands the policy `lead_state_at`'s speed at `action_t + lead_age`, ~0.25 s of it, which is the delay match the bullet below describes.) This credit buys the onset and it buys the stopping margin on a *sustained* brake, which is the part that decided its length. What the credit costs is in the constant's own comment — the regulator's equilibrium moves `(K_V / K_GAP) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T * a_lead` closer, 1 m per m/s² of lead accel, held only while the estimate says the lead is still gaining. Measured: a settled follow behind a lead holding 0.5 m/s² sits 0.35 m closer than the setpoint where the uncredited law sits 0.13 m behind it, and a lead that launches at 1 m/s² for 3 s then brakes at −3 to rest ends 6 cm *further* back — the transient does not survive the approach. Against a hard launch it is worth little alone — a 2 m/s² lead puts the ask above the comfort ramp, so 0.45 m/s² at 0.25 s is all either arm delivers and the credit's own contribution is 0.005 m of gap by 2 s — while a slow lead needs no help: a 0.5 m/s² launch draws 0.292 m/s² at 0.25 s against 0.165, and 0.15 m of gap by 2 s. On top of the launch jerk the two together reach 1.075 m/s² at 0.25 s against 0.672, and 0.42 m of gap by 2 s. The channel is real but not the only one: `lead_state_at`'s projection below still carries the same accel into the *gap*, which is why a launch answers at all when the jerk limit is the binding constraint. `test_the_lead_accel_credit_splits_by_direction_and_by_term` pins both halves by arithmetic at states where each governs, `test_a_launch_is_answered_through_the_projection_and_the_regulator` the two positive channels, and `test_noise_is_averaged_not_amplified` the estimator that feeds them. Measured closed-loop beside that equilibrium: stop-and-go launches from the 6 m standstill target with the lead gaining at 1/2/3 m/s² hold minimum live-target errors of −0.280/0/0 m with the gap floor at 6.0, the reverse closing case (ego 15, lead 8, 28 m gap) shows a largest inward error of 0.222 m, and settled follows at 0.5/1.0 m/s² sit −0.30/−0.33 m recovering in 1.4/0.1 s — every taper tried increased return overshoot or weakened the pinned launch response, so no positive-side taper ships.
- **The up-jerk is scheduled on speed, the down-jerk never is, and the first step of a ramp is the comfort step.** `jerk_limit` reads `MOONPILOT_JERK_LAUNCH` at and below `MOONPILOT_JERK_LAUNCH_SPEED`, tapering to `MOONPILOT_JERK_UP` by twice it — but only once `a_prev` is off zero: 0.075 m/s² cannot cross `should_stop`'s 0.1 release threshold in one frame and 0.2 can, so the first-step rule is what keeps a parked car's brake hold out of a single frame of a lead's reported speed, which is the same property the `trailing` gate protects from the state machine's side (`test_the_stop_gate_follows_the_governing_candidate_not_every_lead`). Measured closed-loop from rest behind a lead pulling away at 2 m/s²: 0.5 m/s² at 0.15 s against 0.30 s on the comfort ramp, and 0.14 m of the gap by 2 s. In that case it is also what makes the anticipation bullet above pay: the credit alone is worth 0.005 m there (the ask is above this ramp either way, so 0.45 m/s² at 0.25 s is all either arm delivers), while the two together are worth 0.56 m, with the ask at 1.075 m/s² by 0.25 s — where a *slow* lead needs no such help, drawing 0.292 m/s² against 0.165 with the credit alone. Scheduling on the speed rather than on a state is what lets the published rollout — which carries its own speed — roll forward under the same rule the command was taken from; the down side is untouched, so every braking number in this section is still the one that was measured. `test_the_up_jerk_is_scheduled_on_speed_and_the_down_jerk_is_not` pins the schedule, the first-step rule and the speed-independence of the down side.

  **Grade coasting is a separate no-lead policy.** `MoonpilotCoastGrade` reads the live vehicle pitch already carried in `carControl.orientationNED[1]` and applies inside the cruise candidate, not in `longcontrol.py`: the controller still tracks one scalar net-acceleration target and therefore handles gravity as a plant disturbance. The fitted `coast_accel` is the no-throttle target; when its road term exceeds `MOONPILOT_COAST_GRADE_MIN` (0.4 m/s², about a 7% grade) and pushes the car away from the set speed, the target tapers toward that coast value inside `MOONPILOT_COAST_BAND` (1.5 m/s, 3.4 mph / 5.4 km/h). Moderate roads stay on exact set-speed control; a high-grade descent can run above the set speed instead of braking immediately, and a high-grade climb can bleed speed instead of adding throttle when throttle is otherwise permitted. Recovery from that sag is not the ladder: just past the climb band edge, in `[band, 2*band)` of underspeed, the positive ask is capped at `MOONPILOT_COAST_RECOVERY_ACCEL` (0.15 m/s²) so it pulls back slow and steady on the same hill — deeper deficits and every other grade keep the plain law, so a launch is unaffected. The descent branch still applies when the model's `allow_throttle` gate is false, because that existing cap never relaxes braking above the set speed; the no-throttle climb path already coasts and remains unchanged. Missing pose, level/sub-threshold grade, feature-off and experimental mode are pass-throughs. Lead, TTC, stopping-floor, curve and model-braking candidates still win through `min`, and the existing jerk limiter smooths the transition. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins both signs, the band edge, the recovery cap and its window, the allow-throttle gates, the closed-loop bounds and the published rollout; its upstream graded maneuvers remain green.
- **The pitch every grade term reads carries a standing offset, and it is learned out.** `carControl.orientationNED[1]` is locationd's calibrated pose and the fork's only grade input, so whatever bias it carries is a hill the planner believes in forever. That bias is measured rather than suspected, on two routes now: the reported pitch is negative on **0.23 %** of 136,440 `carControl` frames with a **+2.27°** median on a 23-segment route, and on **0.95 %** of 7,913 gated frames with a **+2.06°** median (mean 0.0353 rad, per-segment medians 1.47–2.38°) on this 14-segment route — positive and multi-degree on this device with the floor never dropping out, so a standing bias rather than terrain. On the 23-segment route, removing its `carState.aEgo` correlation (r = 0.145, so not body pitch under acceleration) leaves +2.29°, and the drive **ends 71 m from where it started** — a GPS fix at both ends — while integrating `sin(pitch)·ds` over the same 7.93 km claims **+298 m of climb**. That is `asin(298/7927)` = **2.16°**, which `coast_accel`'s road term reads as **−0.21 m/s² of permanent uphill**; its cost is the descent branch, since `_coast_applies` needs a negative pitch and a real descent therefore has to exceed ~6.3° before the band can open at all (Toyota's own `sin(min(pitch, 0))·g` is clamped to zero for the same reason). `moonpilot/pitch.py` learns it as the long-window mean of the pitch while the car is driving — `MOONPILOT_PITCH_RC` 600 s, above `MOONPILOT_PITCH_MIN_SPEED`, trusted after `MOONPILOT_PITCH_MIN_SAMPLES` — and the planner subtracts `applied()` before `coast_accel` sees it. Four things about it. It learns **whenever the car is driving, not only while engaged**, because the bias belongs to the device and the car rather than to who is steering, which is exactly why it is measurable where `moonpilot/latency.py`'s estimate is not: the same corpus holds ~2.4 min of engaged longitudinal control against 136k pose frames. Its **bound is sized above the bias, not against the gate** — `MOONPILOT_PITCH_OFFSET_MAX` is 3°, over the measured 2.16°, because a clamp that binds on a real device saturates silently and under-corrects forever, and `moonpilot/tests/test_pitch.py` pins that ordering so the tempting fix of tightening it is refused. The ceiling on the other side is named rather than argued away: a *wrongly* saturated offset is `sin(3°)·5.65` = 0.296 m/s² of road term, which does open the coast band on level road wherever `MOONPILOT_COAST_GRADE_MIN` sits below that, and what bounds the overspeed there is `MOONPILOT_COAST_BAND`. Reaching it takes a window that is one long climb. It **persists in `MoonpilotPitchOffset`** because the window is longer than most drives: replayed through that route at plannerd's cadence it crosses `estimated` at 300 s and reaches 1.30° of the 2.16° truth over the route's 555 s of driving, which is `1 − exp(−555/600)` — under-converged, therefore under-corrected, which is the conservative direction, and it finishes across drives rather than restarting at zero each boot. From a zero start on this 14-segment route the same shape repeats exactly: the replay lands at 0.0170 rad, which is `mean×(1−e^(−396/600))` — the RC=600 s filter behaving as designed — while the persisted `MoonpilotPitchOffset` is 0.0159 rad (0.91°), under half this route's 0.0353 rad mean, leaving ~0.19 m/s² of phantom uphill in `coast_accel`; but the value is young, not stuck: the pitch feature reached the device hours before this drive, the param mtime falls inside the drive span, and a zero-start replay reproduces the persisted 0.0159 to four decimals — a first full drive from a zero start, so the RC must not be shortened on this evidence; re-check after ~30 min of accumulated valid driving, which should read ≥ 0.030. And the assumption is that **routes come back down**: a window that is one long climb biases it, bounded by the clamp. No row in **The registry** — a learned value is not a behavior the driver flips. End to end on a genuine 5° descent reported through that bias, the planner brakes at −0.625 m/s² uncorrected and coasts at +0.093 with the offset learned, against +0.0938 if the pitch had been truthful.
- **The two inputs are not the same age, and they were being projected as if they were.** `action_t` carries the actuator delay and one model period, applied to the ego and the leads alike — but `radarState` lands about a full cycle behind the `modelV2` tick that polls it. Measured from the logs over 22.8k planner ticks on 25 routes: median age at the tick is **46.2 ms for the lead against 4.1 ms for `carState`**, and radar is the staler input on **100 %** of ticks there (lead-lag median 41 ms, p95 46 ms, never negative). On this 14-segment route the median agrees within 1.2 ms — 47.4 ms over 16,058 ticks — but the positive fraction reads 99.98 %: 3 negative ticks to −2.9 ms, at segment boundaries. So on every tick the planner compared a fresh ego against a gap from ~46 ms ago, and the closing distance in that interval was simply lost. `update` now ages the lead by exactly `modelV2.logMonoTime - radarState.logMonoTime` — from the stamps the SubMaster already carries, so no new constant and nothing to calibrate — and applies both halves of that interval: the lead's own travel rides on the extra prediction time (`action_t + lead_age`), the ego's on `x_stale = v_ego * lead_age`. The published rollout takes the same correction, so the plan is the prediction the command was taken from rather than a fresher one. It cannot cost safety *where the ego can close* at the age that actually occurs, but that is now a measurement rather than a structural guarantee. The old proof was structural because the old floor ignored `a_lead` entirely; `stopping_decel` reads it, and `lead_state_at` decays it, so an aged lead can read as braking less and therefore needing less. Re-swept in the claim's own scope — a lead **not faster than the ego and not accelerating** (`v_lead <= v_ego`, `a_lead <= 0`), 78,260 states per age, `lead_accel` on the aged against the unaged `lead_state_at` exactly as `update` builds it — the shipped law is **0 of 78,260 at 46 ms**, the measured median lead age, and the pre-fix floor is 0 at every age. It goes non-zero only past ages this pipeline does not produce: 1,218 states (1.56 %) at 0.2 s and 7,563 (9.66 %) at 0.5 s. Every one of them is admission flicker rather than a shallower brake — 160–300 m gaps where the fresh floor sits a thousandth past the −1.0 gate and the aged one de-admits, handing the slot to the regulator's large positive ask (worst: `v_ego` 25, gap 300, `v_lead` 21, `a_lead` −8, fresh −1.012 → aged +72.8) — so the exposure is the gate's discontinuity at ranges where nothing is asking for braking yet, and `commIssue` disengages long before 0.5 s of radar age. Outside the claim's scope (a lead faster than the ego, or accelerating) both arms differ in both directions and always have. The closed-loop stop stays essentially the fresh one as staleness grows (stopping at a stationary lead from 25 m/s: 5.2583 m fresh, 5.2557 m at the measured 46 ms, 5.2179 m at 0.5 s, against 4.6356 m uncorrected at 0.5 s). The alternative — folding a sensor-latency term into `action_t` — was measured and is wrong, because `action_t` projects the ego too: it made the same case *worse* (0.17 m of margin lost uncorrected, 0.28 m with the uniform bump), which is why this ages one stream rather than advancing both. A missing or equal stamp gives the zero correction, which is what keeps the maneuver plant's bare dict (it has no `logMonoTime` at all) and upstream's harness behaving exactly as before. It is also the one piece of this fork validated end to end rather than in simulation: replaying real logged messages through the planner on their own `logMonoTime` gives the same ages the sweep predicted — 40.4, 40.6, 45.2 and 46.2 ms median, max 146.6 ms, across the four segments the corpus has with engaged longitudinal control, a lead present on 500–1200 of each segment's ~1200 ticks — and the correction moves the command by at most 0.175 m/s² there, with no NaN and nothing out of the actuator's range over 4797 real ticks. The other half of the chain stays uncompensated on purpose, and that is measured rather than assumed: the whole 255-segment corpus contains only 4 segments with any engaged longitudinal control (13,207 of 1.14 M `carControl` messages, one car, `TOYOTA_RAV4_TSS2`), and its command is quasi-static — median |Δaccel| is 0.000 per 10 ms sample and only 2.5–5.8 % of engaged samples move more than 0.02 m/s² — so jerk-domain, event-pooled and first-order-plus-delay estimators all peak at the noise floor with best lags scattering past causality (−310 ms). Amplitude exists, bandwidth does not. `CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay` = 0.15 s is therefore comma's own calibrated default, not a fork finding, and a corpus that could test it needs sustained *changing* long-control, not merely more engaged minutes.
- **Delay compensation is state prediction, not plan inversion.** The policy is evaluated where the car will be at `longitudinalActuatorDelay + DT_MDL` (`action_t`) and each lead at that plus its own age — `lead_age`, the bullet above — instead of inverting the published plan through `get_accel_from_plan`. That inverse divides by the delay, so a step in the plan comes out amplified by `action_t / dt` — measured at 45 m/s³ and instant `ACCEL_MIN` saturation in simulation, which is why this is the fork's mechanism.
- **The actuator delay it projects through is measured, not assumed.** `action_t` is `max(CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay, the live estimate) + DT_MDL`. The estimate comes from `moonpilot/latency.py` — `lagd`'s machinery (a masked normalized cross-correlation over a 90 s window, then a block average) driven by this chain's own signal pair, the planner's published `a_target` against `carState.aEgo`, with its own ROI and gates: 0.05–0.60 s, a 0.5 m/s² excitation floor on *both* signals, ≥25 s of valid samples in the window, and a 2 s hold-off marking the frames after any invalid one. It is fed only where the fork planner's command is what moves the car — engaged, in `pid`, above 5 m/s, and not at a standstill, on a pedal or under `forceDecel` — so an unengaged stretch or a driver override teaches it nothing. `CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay` is upstream's `0.15` for every car without an override, marked `# TODO estimate car specific lag` (`opendbc/car/interfaces.py`), so this is the only thing that can move it on a car like the 2021 RAV4 XLE; upstream's own lag for the *lateral* chain is already measured live by `lagd` and read by both torque controllers, which is why this is longitudinal-only. **The live value may only lengthen the projection**: the maneuver suite and the stopping floor's bounds were validated at or above the stock constant, and the estimator's own failure modes bias it low — a command that never moves, a long steady ramp (measured: a linear ramp reports the ROI's *floor*, 0.05 s, at `corr` 1.0), all of which `max` makes inert. What it takes to reach a usable estimate is a full window plus 5 blocks of 5 s (25 s) — and, on a car whose engagement comes in short stretches, more than one drive. The block count rides with the value in `MoonpilotLongLagBlocks` (`INT`), so a drive that earns a block or two hands them on instead of restarting; the carried mean is applied only once the blocks are there, and both params are read at construction (a value written before the count existed was only ever written when trusted, so a missing count still seeds as trusted). `MoonpilotLongLag` itself is a `FLOAT`, read with `return_default=True`, whose `"0.0"` default is below the ROI floor and so never seeds. Nothing else changes: `_trajectory` rolls forward from the current state and never reads `action_t`, and `policy`, the candidates, the jerk limits and `publish` are untouched. `moonpilot/tests/test_latency.py` pins the recovery of a known dead time (0.30 s read back to within one frame, and applied), the "may only lengthen" clamp, the two inert cases — a quasi-static command and a constant `aEgo`, the latter of which is why every existing fork test and the maneuver harness (whose plant never sets `carState.aEgo`) behave exactly as before — the recovery hold-off, that an inconsistent block spread is not applied, and the wiring in both directions. The corpus already on this machine says what to expect from it and what the contingency is not: over 199 segments off the car and 42,980 estimate attempts it stays `unestimated` everywhere — the intended outcome on a corpus that cannot support the measurement — because **98.4 % of attempts die on `num_okay`**, too little engaged longitudinal control inside one window (the corpus holds ~2.4 min in total), with the excitation-range gate binding nothing and only 24 estimates ever accepted against the 100 a first trusted block needs. More engaged driving is the lever; a lower `MOONPILOT_LAG_RANGE` is not. Replayed per *drive* — the estimator spans log segments, and feeding it one segment at a time restarts it every minute — those two engaged routes split 3,688 attempts as **82 % short of valid samples, 14 % failing the confidence gate and 4 % the NCC gate**, and the attempts that clear both quality gates read 0.245 s (sd 0.016) and 0.350 s (sd 0.043): right where it can speak, and **1 block of the 5** a trusted mean needs. Sweeping the sizing constants changes nothing (window 40/90/120/180 s × valid gate 5/15/25 s × speed gate 1/2/5 m/s all end at 0 trusted blocks, best case 1), which is what the carried count is for: replayed as five such drives, the value alone ends `unestimated` with one block every time, while carrying the count reaches a trusted 0.366 s on the fifth. Where it did run it reported 0.33–0.40 s at NCC 0.965–0.986, corroborated by an independent high-pass lag scan peaking at 0.34 s, against this car's 0.15 s constant — so the constant is measurably short, but 99.8 % of the command's variance in that corpus is slower than 2 s, the raw correlation is flat across every lag, and the confidence gate lands on its threshold, so a sharp on-device number is not to be expected. The same number reaches `modeld`'s `long_action_t` (`applied_long_delay`, re-read every `MOONPILOT_LAG_MODEL_REFRESH_FRAMES`), so the model's ask is decoded at the horizon the planner projects through rather than the car's stock constant — the two are otherwise computed for different horizons whenever the estimate has moved. No row in **The registry**: a learned value is not a behavior the driver flips, and it needs no dependency the device does not already ship.
- **The lead's acceleration is the fork's own estimate, not radard's.** `aLeadK` is a `KF1D` with hardcoded gains at `DT_MDL` (closed-loop |λ| = 0.903, τ ≈ 0.49 s): 50 % of a −3 m/s² step at 0.60 s, 90 % at 1.20 s. `moonpilot/lead.py`'s `LeadAccelEstimator` takes the least-squares slope of `vLead` over a 0.75 s window (15 samples) instead — a clean ramp reads exactly once three samples are on it (0.10 s), a step onto a window that is already full in that window's own length (0.70 s), and it averages noise rather than lagging it: 0.06 m/s² of jitter over the full window at 0.05 m/s of measurement noise, though that white-noise figure is optimistic on real radar: against a ±1 s centered reference on 18,350 steady-lead frames of 340 local segments the estimate's error is 0.31 m/s² sd, because track churn keeps the window short and the reference carries real sub-second lead motion — and falls back to `aLeadK` for a vision-only lead (where it is the model's own unfiltered accel) and whenever the window cannot speak: fewer than three samples, a new `radarTrackId`, a source flip, or a `vLead` jump past 2.5 m/s in a frame. **The onset window is what stops that window from being the brake-onset delay**: `MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_FAST`'s three newest samples are read beside the full window and believed only when they read `MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_FAST_MARGIN` deeper for `MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_FAST_STREAK` consecutive frames — one-sided by construction, since the policy only ever uses `a_lead` to *add* braking (`min(a_lead, 0.0)` in the preview, and the floor/TTC terms key off the closing rate that follows), and the streak is the noise gate. **The streak is four frames, not two**: a single `vLead` blip-and-return (a +0.2 m/s sample gone the next frame) sits in the three-sample window for three frames, and its falling edge read −1.0…−1.6 m/s² for two or three of them — reads that reach the command through `v_lead_eff` and the lead's projected state as single-frame brake taps while following. On route `000003f8` segments 42–45 (2.0 min of engaged steady follow) a two-frame streak gave 69 frames past −0.8 m/s² and a command high-frequency sd of 0.141; four gives 35 and 0.114, within 0.002 of having no onset window at all, for 0.10 s more onset latency on the plan tail and FCW. It takes a −3.5 m/s² step from 0.40 s to 0.20 s at −2.0 m/s² and from 0.55 s to 0.20 s at −3.0 (estimator level), and cost, at the two-frame streak, against a ±1 s centered reference over 18,350 steady-lead frames of 340 local segments: 0.284 → 0.307 m/s² of error sd and 0.68 % → 1.00 % of frames reading past −1 m/s² — four frames believes the window on a subset of those frames. **The window is 15 samples, not 7, because its noise was the command's.** Replayed open-loop over those segments (22.6 min of closing approaches), the jerk-limited command's jitter (RMS about a 1 s mean) went 0.090 → 0.082 m/s²; smoothing the estimate downstream instead delayed real brakes into contact (a 0.6 s filter hit a lead braking −5 m/s² to rest). No closed-loop brake flown (−0.75 to −5 m/s², settled and mid-approach, a −5 tap) reaches a braking threshold more than 0.05 s later than on 7 samples, and minimum gaps agree within 0.02 m. The gain is capped by radar track churn — `radarTrackId` changes 3.1 times a second in those approaches, 98 % of them the same vehicle, each resetting the window — and keeping the window across same-vehicle changes lowers the estimate's error (0.307 → 0.236) without lowering the command's jitter, so the reset stays. At the command level the onset window is worth nothing measurable today: a −3.5 m/s² brake from a settled 20 m/s follow draws −0.5 / −1.0 / −2.0 m/s² at 0.30 / 0.55 / 0.90 s with or without it, on either window length, so what it buys is the published plan's tail and the FCW, both of which read `a_lead` directly. **The sensing side is no longer the binding constraint, and that is measured rather than assumed**: replacing the estimator with the lead's *true* accel in the closed loop left the command's crossings for a −3.5 m/s² onset exactly where they were when this was measured (−0.5, −1.0 and −2.0 m/s² at 0.20, 0.30 and 0.50 s on the planner of that time; today's planner reads 0.30, 0.55 and 0.90 s, pinned at ≤ 0.35 / 0.60 / 1.00 by `test_a_braking_lead_reaches_the_command_from_its_speed_history`), so what remains is the ramp and the lead's own closing-rate build-up. Against that onset the command reached −1.0 m/s² at 0.30 s and −2.0 m/s² at 0.50 s when measured — 0.55 s and 0.90 s on today's planner, against 0.70 s and 2.65 s for its forced-zero arm per that test — against 0.55 s and 1.15 s then for the same planner reading radard's `aLeadK`/`aLeadTau` off the message (the pinned test's shape, where every lead carries `a_lead=0.0` and radard's value is therefore absent entirely). A *bare* TTC term still costs this — 0.55 s and 2.20 s, because it keys off the closing rate, which only grows once the lead's speed has fallen — and the stopping floor is what gives it back, since a lead braking in front of a settled follow puts `a_stop` past the approach decel early; the braking credit is what makes it early. So the gap between the estimator and radard's arms, which is what this mechanism exists for, is 0.25 s at −1.0 m/s² and 0.65 s at −2.0 m/s². Compared with the same floor with no credit at all, the gains are 0.25 s and 0.45 s; the lead's *speed* stays radarState's raw `vLead`: it has no filter lag to remove — both arms measure the speed channel identically — and the same fit's endpoint value adds a transient bias exactly at the onset of braking. `aLeadTau` is radard's rule — 1.5 s until |a| passes 0.5 m/s², then a filter toward 0, re-armed to 1.5 whenever \|a\| falls back under the threshold or the `radarTrackId` changes — but driven by the fork's own estimate rather than by radard's `aLeadK`. That matters because the two disagree exactly at a brake onset: radard's Kalman is still under the reset threshold while the fork's estimate is already deep, so the message pairs a sustained-looking accel with a 1.5 s decay and the *published plan's* 2.5 s tail decays the braking away — under the kinematic term the tail contradicted the command for 0.25–0.40 s after the onset, worst +0.24 m/s² against a −1.8 m/s² command, which driving the rule off the fork's estimate halved to 0.20–0.25 s. The pinned sample moved to 0.80 s past the onset for margin: there the fork's own decay (0.278 in that scenario) puts the deepest published point at −1.68 m/s² where radard's 1.5 s decay of the *same* accel has already decayed it to −0.01, a separation of 1.674, against 0.629 at the old 0.30 s sample. Both arms run the estimator's accel and differ only in `aLeadTau`, so what moves the plan is the decay and nothing else. What the pairing was fixing is unchanged underneath: `exp(-aLeadTau t²/2)` is radard's assumption that a lead's accel is a transient, and at 2.5 s that assumption is what is wrong — at the pinned sample's deepest point the same accel reads −1.68 m/s² on the fork's own 0.278 s decay, −2.62 m/s² with `aLeadTau` forced to 0 (no decay at all), and −0.01 m/s² on the message's 1.5 s. Severity context: `longitudinalPlan.accels`/`speeds` has no consumer in openpilot outside `test/process_replay/migration.py`, so this whole tail question is log fidelity, not vehicle behavior.
- **It is chosen once, at construction, and only exists for `openpilotLongitudinalControl` cars.** Both seams read the param once, so the toggle takes a restart — the feature description says so, and the row is offroad-only. A car whose stock ACC owns acceleration never reaches the planner seam, and the controller seam is picked there too but inert: controlsd's `CC.longActive` requires `CP.openpilotLongitudinalControl`, so `moonpilot_longcontrol`'s instance stays in `LongCtrlState.off` and returns `0.0`, exactly as upstream's does.
- **`moonpilot_longitudinal_active()` is that same decision without building a planner, and the UI reads it.** Both road views widen the displayed camera to the wide road camera below their speed threshold (`WIDE_CAM_MAX_SPEED` — 10 m/s tizi, 5 m/s mici, with upstream's own hysteresis back to the narrow cam above `ROAD_CAM_MIN_SPEED`) when the fork planner is the one in place, and upstream's own `selfdriveState.experimentalMode` alone no longer decides it: the seam is `... or moonpilot_longitudinal_active(ui_state.CP, ui_state.params)`. The predicate is the factory's own test — toggle on and `CP.openpilotLongitudinalControl` — so the camera can never widen for a planner that was not built, and it costs the render loop no planner. Three properties worth naming. It is a **mode, not an engagement**: like upstream's experimental mode it does not wait for `selfdriveState.enabled`, so the driver sees the same view engaged or not. It is **disjoint from the experimental-mode gate** rather than competing with it — an `openpilotLongitudinalControl` car is exactly the car where experimental mode is unavailable (`ExperimentalMode` is gated on the opposite car property), so this adds a widened camera only where upstream's gate could never have opened it, and no car is in a state where the two disagree about wanting a wider view. And it is **the mode the driver opted into**, which is what makes it the fork's counterpart of the experimental-mode gate: both say "you asked for different behavior here", and neither is a driving input. `CP` is `None` until `carParams` lands, which reads as not-yet rather than as a verdict on the car. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py`'s `TestPlannerActivePredicate` pins the agreement with the factory over every combination, the `None` case, and that both trees carry the seam — two files, one decision, and either alone would leave a device showing a camera its planner never asked for.
- **`MoonpilotLeadLateral` reaches it through the time gap**, not through an MPC danger factor: the fork planner never calls `lead_danger_factor`, so a lead predicted to leave the path scales `t_follow` (by `MOONPILOT_OUT_OF_PATH_T_FOLLOW`) rather than relaxing a constraint in a solver it does not have. **That prediction is a latched two-state claim, not a continuous scale.** `inPath` is a weak signal at highway speed — measured over 689 paired samples on route 000003df (segments 5, 13, 17, 18), in-lane leads (|yRel| < 1.8 m, n=621) read median **0.54** / p10 **0.23**, adjacent-lane leads (n=50) median **0.13** / p10 **0.02**, and `corr(|yRel|, inPath)` is only **−0.22** — and mapping it linearly gave an in-lane lead a permanent **1.10-1.30 s** headway where the personality asks 1.45: a 36 m target silently delivered as 28-33 m, breathing with the estimate. A moving target under a proportional regulator is its own fault, independent of the ladder. So the reduction requires `inPath < MOONPILOT_OUT_OF_PATH_ENTER` (0.10, where it fires on 4.2 % of in-lane leads against 46.9 % of adjacent-lane ones — the best ratio available; 0.20 gives 7.6 % against 63.3 %, 0.30 gives 15.6 % against 67.3 %, and above 0.40 the in-lane false-fires double), and latches until `inPath` clears `MOONPILOT_OUT_OF_PATH_LEAVE` (0.30). The band between them is the hysteresis, wide enough that `inPath`'s own jitter cannot cross it. The failure direction is the safe one: `inPath` that says nothing leaves the car at the personality's own headway, and the only thing the feature can fail to do is close up. **What reaches it is gated on the model's own confidence**: a *vision-only* slot whose filtered prob is under `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PROB_GATE` (0.5 — the threshold radard itself refuses a vision lead at) is published as the empty trajectory, so a phantom slot is neither drawn nor allowed to scale the gap, and `present=False` is upstream's own state to fall back to: no lead, `inPath` 1.0, base `t_follow`. The filter is the point — slot 0 crosses that threshold in runs whose median is 4 frames and 29 % of which are one frame long (corpus, 47k frames), so a raw gate would blink at 20 Hz; `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PROB_RC` is radard's own 0.2 s decay with an instant rise, and `leadd` owns the per-slot filters beside the inPath ones. Two things are deliberately *not* gated: a fused slot, whose prob is radard's already-filtered number for a lead radard decided to publish, and the low-speed radar-override slot, which is a track radard saw rather than a model trajectory. That mattered because nothing gated any of it before — measured by running `normalize_lead` over the corpus, the slot was `present` on 100 % of frames, the renderers drew 87.6 % of them, and of those drawn **38.1 % were under the gate and 38.6 % had no radar-confirmed lead at all**. **The UI side is a line, not the old ribbon**: both trees stop at `MOONPILOT_INPATH_HORIZON` (4 s, the last sample the inPath grid is evaluated at, so the drawn extent is the acted-on extent — the old 10 s tail ran past the white path's own 100 m cap on samples the model rates ±1 m) and take the width of each segment from the model's per-sample `yStd` (`MOONPILOT_LEAD_PATH_PX_PER_M`, clamped to `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PATH_WIDTH`), so what thickens is what the model is unsure about instead of a fixed 1 m band fading out.
- **Stale lead visuals follow the same authority as planning.** Both renderers clear the lead line whenever `moonpilotState` is invalid or its publisher is dead, so a stale observation cannot stay on screen after the planner stops acting on it.
- **The speed it plans from can be corrected**, by `MoonpilotSlam` — see `moonpilot/docs/slam.md`. It arrives as one added number on `v_ego`, so the pass-through case (feature off, nothing published, invalid, stale, publisher dead) is the stock planner exactly.
- **`MoonpilotCurveSpeed` is three bounded braking terms on the model's *predicted* path, all inside the cruise slot.** A kinematic pre-brake against the curve's own distance — the shape the lead stopping floor had before it moved to the relative frame, `(v_target² - v_ego²) / 2d` — which is right here, since a curve does not drive away over every admitted sample, the deepest winning — a jerk-limited target speed, and a proportional in-curve regulator on the lateral accel the car is *actually* pulling. They enter as minima of the cruise candidate (`policy(..., curve=, x_ego=, v_hold=)`), which is what keeps `LongitudinalPlanSource.cruise` truthful — the enum has no curve member and one is not added — and what makes them live in experimental mode too: `min` means each can only ever *add* braking to whatever the model asked for, never substitute for it. The pre-brake is kinematic rather than a plain speed cap because there is a *distance* to the event while the car is outside the curve, and the in-curve term is proportional for the opposite reason: once inside, there is no distance left to plan against, only the curvature the steering is already commanding. **The pre-brake's distance is floored at `MOONPILOT_CURVE_MIN_T` (1.0 s) of travel**, so a sample the car is about to reach asks about `(v_target - v_ego) / MOONPILOT_CURVE_MIN_T` instead of `(v_target² - v_ego²) / 2d` with `d` going to zero — a ratio that is the model path's sample noise within a few meters: on route `000003f8` segment 0 (~47 s) a binding sample a few meters out and 0.6 m/s under the car's speed asked the −1.5 floor on alternate frames for half a second. Swept on that segment against the late sharp curve there (a ~3 m/s² apex at 12.7 m/s, which wants the whole −1.5 floor) and that flicker: 0.5 s left the flicker at −0.8…−1.2, 1.67 s (the in-curve gain's own time) halved the sharp curve's braking to −0.86, and 1.0 s keeps that curve at −1.2…−1.43 while the flicker reads −0.15…−0.6. Replaying the route's logged inputs through the planner, frames with a pre-brake under −1 on the engaged curves went 54 → 17, all of them the genuine late sharp curve. Beyond that distance the law is unchanged, and a large overspeed still saturates at the floor. The term's own floor is what the preview has to fit inside, and at the top of the range it does not: flown closed-loop from 25 m/s at a 250 m-radius curve, on a 250 m-radius curve the pre-brake is bounded by the term's own floor when the 4 s preview cannot cover the required speed reduction, and the in-curve regulator takes over once the car is inside; the closed-loop arrival is pinned by the curve tests rather than by a route-specific number. The pre-brake's distance is measured from the car now, not from the frame the model saw: `path_age` is the age at the planner tick that consumes the message, not its publish stamp — msgq buffers, so the tick is when the planner actually reads it — with the publish lag (31 ms median, 34 p95, 80 max over 244 corpus segments, 143k frames) as the fallback for a clock that cannot speak (a replay); what the publish stamp misses is the plan's own publish-to-send interval, 15.9 ms median over 282,554 corpus plans, of which only 0.58 ms is the solve. It moves the curve's `x` back to the car now by `v_ego · path_age` (`curve_targets`' `ahead`) in both the command and the published rollout — over the full 255-segment corpus it deepens 3.2 % of the frames where the curve term lowers the command and shallows none of them (1,302 binding frames), the shallower direction being the entry the car has already passed, which is the in-curve term's business, and the tick anchor's own extra interval moves 37 of those frames, by a median 0.010 and at most 0.089 m/s² — bounded by `MOONPILOT_CURVE_PATH_MAX_AGE`; absent, zero or impossible stamps give the zero shift this planner had before the correction existed.
- **`MoonpilotSqueeze` is the corridor strip's cruise-slot consumer, and it is a fourth minimum beside the curve terms.** `update` reads `modelV2.roadEdges` each frame (gated on the offroad-only toggle). When a valid `moonpilotState.egoPose` is published the free-space strips are pose-stitched over a short history (`RollingCorridor`, 1.5 s / 40 frames, relative SE2 warp into the current pose, free-region intersection) so a single frame's edge dropout does not release the brake; without a pose (SLAM off, first fix, dead publisher) it clears and falls back to the single-frame `moonpilot.corridor.squeeze_accel` — exactly the planner this fork had before the history existed. Either path returns `ACCEL_MAX` — no candidate — when the edges are absent, empty, unknown over the window, or already wider than `MOONPILOT_SQUEEZE_FULL_WIDTH` (4 m, ~2 fixed car widths; `CarParams` has no width field). Past that threshold a linear ramp floors at `MOONPILOT_SQUEEZE_ACCEL_MIN` (−1.5, the curve bound) by `MOONPILOT_SQUEEZE_MIN_WIDTH` (2 m, one body), over the next `MOONPILOT_SQUEEZE_LOOKAHEAD` (40 m) of known edge after the same path re-reference the curve terms use. `min` again means it can only add braking to whatever else won the cruise slot, and the source stays `cruise`. No road edges — the default `_inputs` and what upstream's maneuver plant fills — is exactly the planner this fork had before, feature on or off; `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal_features.py`'s `TestSqueeze` pins that inertness and the toggle, `test_corridor.py` the pure math and the pose-stitched history.
- **`MoonpilotPathOutside` is the corridor's second consumer, and it is a banner not a brake.** `path_outside_alert` in `moonpilot/corridor.py` is called from selfdrived beside the turn-desire banner: feature gate, then `carControl.latActive` (so a disengaged or half-engaged-off car does not get a banner for a path nobody is following), then `path_outside_fraction` over the model's own path against `roadEdges`. Unknown samples (edges do not span that x) are excluded from both numerator and denominator — a path the model has not painted edges for is not evidence the path left the road — and the threshold is `MOONPILOT_PATH_OUTSIDE_MIN_FRAC` (0.25 of known samples in `MOONPILOT_PATH_OUTSIDE_LOOKAHEAD`, 40 m) so one noisy sample does not warn. Raising `EventName.pathOutside` (`log.capnp` `@108`) is the whole seam; the alert is `AlertStatus.userPrompt`, small, low priority, with `AudibleAlert.prompt` for 1.5 s. Distinct from LDW (lane lines / desire) and from the turn-desire banner (blinker): this is the model's own trajectory leaving the free corridor. Off by default — the fraction threshold is not validated on a car until someone drives it.
- **The jerk ceiling is what makes a tightening entry slow earlier rather than harder.** Lateral jerk at constant speed is `v³·|dκ/ds|`, so a ceiling on jerk *is* a ceiling on speed — `cbrt(J_LAT / |dκ/ds|)` — and that target falls as the curvature ramps in, which is the one thing a measured lateral-accel limit cannot see. `|dκ/ds|` is a centered difference over `MOONPILOT_CURVE_JERK_STEP` = 2 m, clamped to the path's own ends and divided by the span it actually used, rather than between the model's own samples — whose spacing runs 0.1 m to 12 m along one path, so a sample-scale gradient's value depends on where it happens to sit, and the pre-brake takes the *deepest* sample of the window, so that choice goes to whichever sample is noisiest. Over 143,058 corpus frames, the frames where only the ceiling asks for braking (741, 0.52 %) split by exactly that: the 33 % a 2 m window drops sit at a median 0.47 m spacing with incoherent steps (net over summed |dκ| 0.42, |κ| at 0.6× its own ±5 m neighbourhood) — a single sample's step, which no road has and the model's own 0.1 s sampling cannot have resolved — while the 67 % it keeps are coherent ramps at 1.7 m spacing with |κ| at 1.7× their neighbourhood, at full depth. A 5 m window was measured too and dropped coherent ramps (the dropped set's coherence median 1.0), so the window is sized to the sub-meter class rather than to the ramp the term exists for, and nowhere in the corpus does it ask for *more* braking than the sample scale did (0 of 143,058 frames). The window is a **scale**, not a coherence filter, and that is its named ceiling: it attenuates structure narrower than itself by roughly the ratio of the two, so at the model's wider highway spacing a sub-meter step is only halved and can still bind — the corpus that measured this runs 8–18 m/s, where the same step sits at 0.47 m and is dropped outright. A guard on the binding sample's own coherence (κ at 1.7× its ±5 m neighbourhood versus 0.6× for the two classes) is what would separate them at every spacing, and it is machinery for its own measured change rather than this one. A path with `dκ/ds = 1e-4` 1/m² caps the entry at `cbrt(3.0/1e-4)` ≈ 31.1 m/s — above the 21.79 m/s a 250 m radius is worth, so on a 40 m ramp the budget governs and the ceiling is inert — while a ramp tight enough to reach `dκ/ds = 4e-4` (10 m) puts the ceiling at 19.6 m/s and it is the ceiling that governs: at 25 m/s braking then begins 93.0 m out against 90.0 m with `MOONPILOT_CURVE_J_LAT` raised to 100, which is the whole of what the term buys. A constant-radius path has no gradient at all and is bounded by the budget alone.
- **A curve's exit rebuilds the positive ask at the driver's own ramp, not at the cruise rung's.** Without it the exit was the cruise ladder's rung behind the 1.5 m/s³ comfort ramp: on route `000003f8` segment 0 (12 m/s, ~3 m/s² of lateral at the apex) the command went −1.50 → +1.17 in 2.0 s and the car pulled +1.35 m/s² one second after the unwind with 20° of wheel still in. The driver's own exits on that route, from comparable apexes at 11–14 m/s, sit near +0.1 at the unwind, +0.25…0.45 one second later and +0.4…0.5 at two; `MOONPILOT_CURVE_EXIT_JERK` (0.25 m/s³) from zero at the release is that ramp. The planner keeps one piece of state for it, `curve_exit_cap`: each update, while any cruise-slot curve term (pre-brake, in-curve hold or corridor squeeze) brakes at the projected state, the cap is held at `max(a_prev, 0)` — zero once the command is braking — and once they all let go it climbs at that jerk to `ACCEL_MAX`, where it is inert; a planner reset puts it back there. `policy(..., exit_cap=)` folds it into `a_curve`, the cruise slot's minimum, so it only caps the positive side and can never add braking or reach a lead term; the published rollout ramps it too, at `exit_cap + MOONPILOT_CURVE_EXIT_JERK · t` per step. With the pre-brake floor above, replaying segment 0's logged inputs through the planner took the engaged curves' command high-frequency sd from 0.247 to ~0.13.
- **The lead-departure time-gap handoff is smoothed symmetrically.** The lateral feature is direction-agnostic: the planner sees only `inPath`, so a lead leaving left or right moves `t_follow` the same way. The reduced out-of-path headway is approached at a bounded rate, while the raw gap, lead speed, TTC and stopping-floor inputs remain immediate. An absent lead resets the gap to the conservative base value; a radar-track flicker is not treated as a new lead. This preserves the tighter gap once a departure is established without letting a track switch turn into an acceleration/braking step.
- **A slower lead's positive follow ask tapers over the last `MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_TAPER_M` (3 m).** The spacing regulator's positive output is scaled down as the gap approaches its nominal target, so acceleration reaches zero before the braking side takes over; a faster lead and every negative/safety term are unchanged. This is the comfort-side half of the approach, not a relaxation of TTC, the stopping floor or the lead's raw speed.
- **The positive side runs on less authority above 20 m/s, because reaching the cruise rung is what made it a step.** `MOONPILOT_POS_AUTH_V` is 1.0 at and below 20 m/s — so every stop, approach and maneuver the fork has been driven on is bit-identical — and falls to 0.35 by 22 m/s (72 to 79 kph) and 0.28 by 30. The multiplier is on the regulator's *output*, and only where that output is positive. Measured on route 000003df segment 17, 53.5-55.5 s: a lead pulling away at ~0.8 m/s² with the gap 1.5 m past its own target read **+0.99**, so `policy`'s `min` handed the whole positive side to the cruise ladder's saturated fast-accel rung — a flat **+0.87 for two seconds**, which is the surge from the seat, while the ego trailed by 0.4-0.95 m/s and the gap opened 3 m. Upstream's MPC, fed the identical `radarState` at the identical tick, read **+0.27..+0.41** and wanted *more* distance than this planner did (33-40 m against 31 m), so the difference was the response shape, not the spacing target. Two properties are load-bearing and both are pinned. It moves the point at which the speed term alone reaches the rung from 1.45 m/s of lag — which was exactly the rung — out to 4.4. And it is the *output* rather than the two terms, because the terms carry opposite signs whenever the ego is both too far back and too fast, and scaling them individually moves that sum toward zero: a state that braked at the `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL` clamp read −0.84 under a 0.35 authority where the pre-gain law read −1.0. An ego in that state is closing on a slowing lead, so that is a weakened brake. The cost is damping — one authority scales the positive region's zeta by `sqrt(f)` and its period by `1/sqrt(f)`, 0.55 to 0.33 and 11 s to 19 s — and it is paid deliberately: a meter or two of slow drift over twenty seconds is a better thing to feel than a two-second step. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal_follow.py` pins the ask, the saturation point, the damping cost, the bit-identical low-speed law and the differential that the braking side never moves.
- **The time gap is not a reason to brake while the ego is the slower car.** `MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_SPEED_FLOOR_HEADWAY_T` (1.0 s) and `MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_SPEED_FLOOR_MIN_A_LEAD` (-0.1 m/s²) bound a floor that keeps the ego's pace instead. Measured cause, on route 000003df segments 17-18: with the set speed at 111 kph and a lead holding 24 m/s, the regulator read **-1.00 — its full approach brake — at 24, 25 and 26 m/s**, i.e. at *exactly* the lead's speed too, and at a 10 m gap with the ego 1 m/s slower, while `stopping_decel` read -0.00 to -0.08 in those same states. Nothing there needed stopping for; the command was a 1.45 s headway the lead itself set, bought with the throttle shut, and the ego's speed decayed to below the lead's and stayed there. Upstream's MPC reads **+0.21** on the identical `radarState` at the identical tick. At the measured state the floor reads 0.00 matched, +0.30 at 0.5 m/s down and +0.60 at 1 m/s down.

  Four gates, two of them fades, and **each was found by a test rather than by argument.** Inert when the ego is the *faster* car — fading out over `MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_SPEED_FLOOR_FADE_V` (0.1 m/s) of excess speed — or a closing approach stops responding to the gap at all (a fly crossed 7.5 m inside a 1 m cushion). Off for a lead that is slowing — fading out over `MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_SPEED_FLOOR_FADE_A` (0.2 m/s²) of braking past -0.1 — because holding the ego's speed into a -3.5 m/s² brake makes the ego relatively *faster* and the gap close instead of open: 2.5 m of end gap on `test_mid_approach_lead_brake_stays_safe` before the arrival term; with the arrival term owning that approach, its peak (-1.61) and minimum and resting gap (8.79 m) are now identical with or without this gate, so there it is a backstop. Off below 1.0 s of headway, where the gap is the safety argument rather than a comfort one; that also keeps the hole narrow, because the fork has no other term that brakes a short headway when the ego is the slower car — TTC is skipped entirely, being a function of closing rate, and the stopping floor is shallow against a lead that is not itself slowing. Off at rest, where the headway test is vacuous and `v_lead_eff` clamps to zero, so the car would otherwise pull away from a stopped lead inside the standstill gap. **The speed and accel gates are fades, not switches, because both sit on the steady follow's own equilibrium**: at a settled follow `v_lead_match - v_ego` and `a_lead` straddle zero by radar noise alone (0.03–0.05 m/s of `vLead`, ~0.1 m/s² of slope), and a hard gate there swapped the floor's ~0 for the gap term's −0.2 on alternate frames (route `000003f8` segment 44). The floor now raises the ask by the product of the two linear fades times what it would add, so it is whole exactly where it was whole before and gone where a real closing or a real brake starts. The speed fade is kept to about two sigma of that noise on purpose: wider, and a car that is genuinely a little faster keeps most of the floor and drifts inside the target instead of being braked back out of it. With the arrival slack floor and the four-frame onset streak, replaying that route's logged inputs through the planner took the steady follow's command high-frequency sd from 0.178 to ~0.12. The floor's value is unscaled on purpose: this is not a comfort quantity, it is "do not lose the lead's speed", and TTC and the stopping floor are `min`'d on top and still own every case that needs braking. What it changes is where the car *rests*: behind the crawl-lead brake it sits at the lead's own 6.50 m/s and 8.79 m (1.35 s of headway), where with the floor off it sits 0.117 m/s below the lead with the gap drifting open (route 000003df: 0.07 m/s below the lead at 9.31 m), and a follow that converges in speed rests where the speeds met — 0.165 m inside a 29 m target on the cushion test — rather than buying that back with speed.
- **A leadless model stop keeps its easing trajectory.** Once model braking is admitted, no lead is present, and speed is at or below `MOONPILOT_MODEL_STOP_ENTER_SPEED` (1.5 m/s), the planner still declares `shouldStop`. While physically rolling, the acceleration controller follows that plan with a small minimum deceleration rather than progressively deepening an independent stop ramp. Physical standstill starts the stationary hold; model release hysteresis still decides when the stop intent ends.
- **`MOONPILOT_CURVE_A_LAT` is 1.7 m/s² from route 000003d7.** The path-budget sweep on that route brought the pre-brake on the first missed curve forward by roughly 3 s and on the later curve by roughly 1.7 s, without a sampled trigger on the intervening mild curve. The change only lowers the speed a curve is worth; the bounded `-1.5 m/s²` floor, bank correction, one-sided learned scale and `min()` arbitration are unchanged.
- **The bank is credited exactly the way `clip_curvature` credits it.** The achievable lateral-accel band is shifted by `g·roll` — `lat_accel_budget`'s one formula, used by every term, numpy-broadcast over the per-sample turn directions — so a banked left-hander is worth more and a right-hander less by the same amount, and a roll large enough to drive the budget under `MOONPILOT_CURVE_A_LAT_MIN` floors there rather than asking for a stop. The in-curve term's budget takes its direction from the sign of the measured curvature.
- **The learned lateral scale is one-sided, and that is the whole safety argument.** `LatAccelBiasEstimator` pairs `|orientationRate.z|·velocity.x` from the model's path — what the plan predicted it would pull — with the measured `curvature·v_ego²` one second later, at `vehicleParameters.valid` car speeds, and only while lateral control is ours (engaged, not `steeringPressed`, moving, not at a standstill); a straight-line frame teaches nothing because both magnitudes must clear `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_MIN_LAT_ACCEL`, and an invalid frame empties the pairing queue so a prediction never scores a measurement from the other side of a disengage. `applied()` is clamped at 1.0 and bounded at `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_MAX`: a car realizing *more* lateral accel than the path predicted is effectively in a tighter curve and should slow more, while the opposite direction would hand the feature *less* braking than the geometry says, which is the one direction this cannot be allowed to fail in. The raw ratio conflates lateral-controller tracking error with genuine model bias and nothing here separates them, which is the second reason the applied value is one-sided. It persists in `MoonpilotCurveLatScale` (`FLOAT`, `"1.0"` neutral, read with `return_default=True`, seeded above 1.0 only) on the estimator's own 1200-frame cadence and gate. No row in **The registry**: a learned value is not a behavior the driver flips. A route sweep (3,744 bias-valid frames) leaves `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_MIN_LAT_ACCEL` at 1.0: all 90 threshold-0.5 pairs come from two curve entries with ratios sweeping 0.65→1.89 within seconds — entry transient, not stable bias — reopened only on plateau-corner pairs with per-maneuver spread within ±0.1.
- **Tracking error is not learned as path bias.** When the torque log is the fork's non-default version, the estimator also requires an active, unsaturated log whose measured and desired lateral acceleration differ by no more than `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_TRACKING_TOLERANCE`; a missing/default log keeps the previous behavior, and an invalid frame clears the pairing window.
- **All of it is inert without the model's path arrays, and the in-curve term additionally without `vehicleParameters.valid`.** `curve_targets` returns `None` — no candidate, i.e. the planner this fork had before this existed — for a message with no `orientationRate`, which is why upstream's maneuver plant (`plant.py` fills `position` and `velocity` but never `orientationRate`) and every existing fork test behave identically whether the feature is on or off; its `vehicleParameters` message is bare, so `valid` is false and the in-curve term cannot engage even where a steer angle is passed in. `paramsd` composes that flag from its own sensor, angle-offset and roll validity, so on a car it means the measurement behind the curvature is trustworthy — the same reason `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py`'s `TestCurveSpeed` group has to set it explicitly. The measurement itself is `-VM.calc_curvature(radians(steeringAngleDeg - angleOffsetDeg), CS.vEgo, roll)`, the idiom `controlsd` and `moonpilot/latcontrol.py` already use, against the raw `CS.vEgo` rather than the slam-corrected `v_ego`: it is a measurement of the car, not a plan input.
- **Should-stop is gated on not trailing a moving lead.** `should_stop(v_ego, a_target)` and its `v_ego < 0.3` speed gate are upstream's, and upstream's planner only reaches those speeds on the way to rest, where declaring a stop is right. The fork's regulator instead trails a lead at the lead's own speed, and there the command sits on zero — inside the predicate's 0.1 threshold — so the flag used to toggle ~13 times a second and `LongControlState.stopping` spent the creep fighting the plan: against a 0.30 m/s lead the car parked 0.37 m behind its own setpoint with 202 mm/s of speed ripple, and the command dithered −0.14…+0.11 where the plan wanted ~0. With the gate the same car sits on its setpoint to three decimals with 1.2 mm/s of ripple. The suppression is narrowed twice, and both narrowings are load-bearing. It fires only when the ego is moving (`v_ego > MOONPILOT_SHOULD_STOP_SPEED`, the fork's standstill convention), so a parked car cannot lose its brake hold to a lead whose reported speed jitters over the deadband. And it keys on the lead that *won* `policy`'s minimum — `source`, matched against the `lead_states` slot — not on every present lead, so a moving `leadTwo` in the next lane while parked behind a stopped `leadOne` cannot lift the flag either; measured, that case alone took the delivered command from `CP.stopAccel` to 0.00 and the car rolled. The same keying is what keeps a force-decel stop intact, since that intent is the *cruise* candidate's (`forceDecel` zeroes `v_cruise`) and no lead slot matches it. Stopped or absent leads — the cases the upstream predicate was written for — are untouched, and so is resume: a lead that pulls away lifts the command over the 0.1 threshold by itself, and both `long_control_state_trans`'s cruise-standstill pin and controlsd's resume read this flag. How long that takes is a property of the lead, not a fixed frame count: the release threshold is `0.3 * dgap + 0.6 * v_lead >= 0.1`, i.e. `dgap + 2 * v_lead >= 0.333 m`, so any lead above ~0.17 m/s clears it on speed alone and the car moves within a frame, while a slower creep has to open that third of a meter of gap first — measured 1.55 s at 0.1 m/s, 5.35 s at 0.05, 16.6 s at 0.02 and 123 s at 0.003, the car sitting still for as long as it takes the lead to get ~0.33 m away, which is the intended reading of "the lead has barely moved". Below ~0.1 m/s of lead speed the gate's own deadband leaves the flag chattering on the 0.1 boundary (measured 20–40 flips in 20 s, pre-existing), i.e. the car inches rather than creeps; that band is the deadband's edge and not a stall. A lead decelerating to rest crosses the deadband and the upstream predicate takes over, so when a genuine stop engages is unchanged and all 60 upstream maneuver combinations still pass. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins the creep closed-loop, the two narrowed-out cases, and that stopped-lead and no-lead still declare the stop.
- **The deadband the stop gate keys on is 12σ of the lead-speed noise at rest, and that was measured rather than assumed.** `MOONPILOT_SHOULD_STOP_SPEED` sits on raw `radarState` `vLead` with no filter behind it, unlike `leadd`'s probability and in-path channels, which reads as a bare threshold on a noisy signal. It is not: over 371 s of corpus where the ego is parked and the lead is provably stationary (`dRel` moving under 5 cm across ±0.5 s, 7,423 frames, 40 segments), `vLead` has sd 0.0084 m/s, max +0.05, and **zero** frames above the 0.1 gate — the channel is quantized at 0.025 m/s and effectively silent at rest, where the moving case is noisier — 0.024–0.035 m/s of `vLead` noise measured across speed bands on the 199-segment corpus. One 2-frame excursion to +0.327 exists in the whole `v_ego < 0.3` band, and a radard-style 0.2 s filter would only take it to +0.114, so filtering buys nothing here. What the corpus cannot speak for is the creep band itself: it holds 2 frames of the ego moving between 0.1 and 0.3 m/s behind a stationary lead, so the gate's behavior there rests on the closed-loop tests rather than on logs.
- **Nothing holds a stop once the lead leaves the slot, and that is upstream's behavior, measured rather than inherited by accident.** With a live set speed and no lead in `radarState`, the cruise candidate is the only one left and it asks to go: flown from rest behind a stopped lead at the standstill gap whose slot then vanishes, the fork commands +1.57 m/s², crosses 0.1 m/s 0.25 s later and reaches the lead's former position in 2.95 s. Upstream's planner in the identical scenario is *faster* — it steps straight to +1.60 on the first frame with no launch ramp and crosses 0.1 m/s at 0.10 s — so a fork-owned guard here would be a divergence, not a fix, and neither planner has any memory of the lead it was holding for. It is also not a case the logs produce: over 44 real stops in the corpus (ego above 5 m/s with a radar-confirmed lead inside 50 m, decelerating to rest, the lead still present at the moment of rest, 556 s stopped) the slot is empty for **2.10 s in total, 0.378 % of stopped time**, in 2 of the 44 stops, worst single gap 1.75 s — and both of those stops are at 30.8 m and 11.7 m of gap, while all 42 stops with a close lead (3–6 m, the geometry where pulling away would hit something) hold `present` at 1.000 for the entire standstill. The flicker that does exist is in *parked* frames rather than stops behind a car (lead present on only 47.6 % of them, 98 dropouts, median 0.55 s), which is clutter coming and going rather than a car being lost. Bridging it on the model would not work either: `leadsV3[0].prob` during those gaps peaks at 0.46–0.499, just under the 0.5 radard itself refuses a vision lead at. If a close-lead dropout ever does show up in logs, the shape to reach for is a short hold of the last governing lead across the gap, not a change to the stop predicate. This 14-segment route agrees and returns no-go on the hold: 145 dropout runs, 140 re-acquired at median 0.41 s; 34 moving-close dropouts, 32 of them track-id swaps to a different object; zero dropouts releasing braking the geometry still calls for; every stop behind a car holding `present` (266/266 and 110/110 at 4.8–5.3 m). Acquisition churn at 40–64 m with the command essentially unmoved was not a reason to add state there; route `000003f8` is the case where far churn did move the command, and the hold below is scoped to it.
- **A far radar lead outlives a lapse in radard's association — and only a far one.** Toyota's radar and the model disagree about a far lead: on route `000003f8` the model's range ran ~14 m short (median) and its speed ±6–7 m/s off (p10/p90), so past ~60 m radard kept failing to match and handed `leadOne` to the vision-only lead (84 % of vision-only leads on that route were past 60 m, median 93 m) or to nothing for 0.25–1.5 s. On segment 42 that alternated a 14 m/s radar track with a 21 m/s vision lead 20 m nearer, and the arrival command stepped −0.5 ↔ +0.1 seven times in four seconds. `_radar_hold` keeps the last radar `leadOne`, projected forward with `lead_state_at`, as an extra `lead0` candidate for `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_T` (2 s) after it stops being the slot; the current lead stays a candidate beside it, so `min` still brakes for a stopped car only vision sees. It is released at once below `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_MIN_V` (5 m/s) or inside `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_MIN_GAP` (40 m), so the bullet above still holds: nothing holds a stop, or a close cut-out. Its `modelProb` is zero, so FCW never fires on a held track.
- **One vehicle is one lead, whichever radar tracks it arrives on.** radard tracks a car as several returns and can match both model leads to the nearest one, so near a stop a single stopped car filled `leadOne` and `leadTwo` on two tracks whose speeds disagreed by a few tenths (route `00000400` segment 4, tracks 517/541, swapping every 0.1–0.3 s). Each swap refitted the lead accel from three samples or fell back to the new track's `aLeadK` (±0.6 m/s² on a car at rest), and `min` over the two readings handed the win back and forth: the command pulsed −0.88 → −1.26 → −0.75 m/s² at 1.9 m/s, and the car followed. `moonpilot.lead.same_car` (within 1 m of range and 1 m/s of speed) now keeps `LeadAccelEstimator`'s window across a track change on the same car, and the planner drops `leadTwo` from the plan's candidates when it is the same car as `leadOne`. FCW still reads both slots: the two matches can carry different `modelProb`, and the dropped one may be the only one over FCW's 0.9 bar. Replayed, that stop's last 4.5 s of command jitter went 0.090 → 0.056 m/s² and the pulse to −0.84 → −1.04 → −0.89; what remains is the one track's own `vLead` noise against the model's candidate, which `min` still arbitrates.
- **Throttle is lost under the model's 0.4 gas-press probability and returns only over 0.5.** Upstream's gate (`allowThrottle`, the coast cap in `cruise_cap`) has no hysteresis, and upstream's MPC smooths what this planner passes straight through: on segment 43 the probability hovered on 0.4 behind a slowing lead, a third of the no-throttle runs lasted one frame (median three), and the command dithered −0.2 ↔ −0.42. The signal is good — in that route's manual driving the driver was on the gas on 3 % of frames under 0.4 and 78 % over it — so `MOONPILOT_ALLOW_THROTTLE_RELEASE` adds hysteresis rather than moving the threshold: engaged no-throttle runs went 27 → 18, one-frame runs 9 → 1.

The acceleration controller keeps upstream's PI/feedforward family and the car's own `longitudinalTuning.kiV` schedule. `long_control_state_trans` remains upstream's contract. In `stopping`, a physically rolling car rate-limits toward `min(a_target, -MOONPILOT_MIN_ROLLING_STOP_DECEL)` (−0.2 m/s² minimum): stronger braking stays reachable and zero or slightly positive stop targets still bring the wheels to rest. Only `CS.standstill`, not the PCM's cruise-standstill bit, starts the `MOONPILOT_STOPPING_JERK` ramp toward `CP.stopAccel`. Acceleration limits apply in either branch. A `stopping → pid` edge retains its negative braking baseline; only a positive plan resets that baseline to zero so the brake hold releases promptly. This removes the repeated brake-release pulse on negative-plan state chatter without adding another stop state machine. At the reproduced signal-stop state, a plan of −0.33 m/s² remains −0.33 while rolling instead of ramping to −1.23. Toyota's downstream PID, permission hysteresis and standstill release are unchanged. Logged-trajectory replay and synthetic plants cannot prove physical brake release or cabin comfort; those require an on-device drive.

One trap is worth naming: `log.LongitudinalPersonality.standard` is a plain `int`, but the same enum read off a message is a capnp `_DynamicEnum` whose hash is not that int, so a dict keyed by the enum's members never matches a value read off `selfdriveState` — `MOONPILOT_T_FOLLOW` is keyed by the raw value for that reason. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins it, along with the properties above, and ends by running upstream's own maneuver suite — all 15 maneuvers × 4 `(e2e, force_decel)` combinations from `openpilot/selfdrive/test/longitudinal_maneuvers/test_longitudinal.py`, patched onto the plant — against the fork planner, which is the end-to-end statement that the strategy drives the stock scenarios without a crash, without stalling at a stop, and while still decelerating under `forceDecel`. `moonpilot/tests/test_longcontrol.py` holds the controller to controlsd's call contract and to the three things the fork owns in it.
