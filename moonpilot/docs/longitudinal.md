# The longitudinal strategy
`moonpilot/longitudinal.py` is the fork's own planner — what acceleration to ask for — and `moonpilot/longcontrol.py` its own acceleration controller, how that acceleration is tracked. Two seams pick them: `moonpilot_longitudinal_planner()` in plannerd, `moonpilot_longcontrol()` in controlsd, both factory calls returning `None` when the toggle is off, so upstream's acados MPC and upstream's PI loop stay on the line as the fallback. The things about them that are not obvious:

- **The cruise candidate is the driver's own ladder, interpolated, not a gain.** `cruise_accel` is `np.interp` of the speed error over `MOONPILOT_CRUISE_ERR_BP` (−5, −3, −1.5, 0, 1, 5 m/s) onto medium brake, light brake, coasting, hold, slow accel and fast accel — each rung measured from ~4 h of this driver's manual longitudinal on the RAV4 (308 segments, grade removed against each segment's median pitch): slow accel is the median of accelerating gas episodes' mean per start-speed band (0.77…0.31 m/s²), fast accel the p75 of *instantaneous* accel at each speed while the episode is still ≥ 5 m/s short of where it ends — the ladder's own fast-rung condition — so it carries a launch's shape rather than its average (0.83 at 0.5 m/s, ~1.97 at 2.5–3.5, 1.27 at 11, 0.87 at 17.5; `cruise_cap`'s combined budget is 2.0 through the launch band and 1.7 from 7 to 20 m/s, so the measured launch peak is what binds): flown from rest the planner reads 0.83 / 1.35 / 1.97 / 1.41 / 1.21 m/s² at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 6 / 8 s and 11.9 m/s at 8 s, against this driver's 0.78 / 1.40 / 2.20 / 0.59 / 0.34 and 12.1 m/s; flown from the states of 31 cached manual launches behind a departing lead it peaks at a median 1.97 at 2.8 s against the driver's 1.92 at 2.8 s, where the former flat 1.7 budget held a trapezoid on the clip. The slow rung's +1 m/s breakpoint is measured too, by taking the plateau a manual speed-up settles on as the driver's target, with no lead within 80 m through the rise or the plateau: over 117 such speed-ups the driver still asks a median +0.19…+0.41 inside the last 0.5 m/s, where a +2 breakpoint tapered to +0.04…+0.06 and reached within 0.5 m/s of the target in a median 9.8 s against the driver's 4.9. At +1 the flown planner reaches it in a median 5.8 s (per flight a median 0.6 s and a p90 2.5 s behind the driver), its median accel curve stays within 0.06 m/s² of the driver's median over the first 6 s while single flights differ by a median 0.39 at their worst instant, and none of the 117 flights overshoots the target (the driver overshoots a median 0.28 m/s). It asks up to ~0.2 m/s² more than the driver at 1–2 m/s of error below 10 m/s, and those samples are not grade-corrected. Coasting is the no-pedal level-road median (−0.10…−0.33), light/medium brake the p90/median of 316 brake episodes' mean decel (−0.75/−1.25). Hard brake (p10, −1.75) is measured and deliberately not a cruise rung. `MOONPILOT_FAST_ACCEL_V` is also `cruise_cap`'s comfort curve, so one table bounds every positive ask. `forceDecel` (`v_cruise` 0) keeps the pre-ladder law floored at medium brake, because the coasting rung would soften driver-monitoring escalation at low speed. Below creep speed (`MOONPILOT_CREEP_SPEED`, 1.9 m/s, where a no-pedal car settles) `crawl_accel` caps the spacing regulator's positive ask at fast crawl (+0.31) unless the lead accelerates at least that hard, fading out by twice creep speed. The two braking crawl rungs — slow crawl −0.08, stopped −0.47 over the last second into a stop (p25 −0.81, p75 −0.26) — are measured and **not** applied. Before `soft_stop`, the unshaped regulator already stopped at that rate: −0.31 over the last second and −0.47 over the two before, resting 5.19 m back from every approach speed 5–25 m/s. Compressing its braking toward the rungs softened the finish further (−0.28) and rested 4.63 m back. The final meters behind a stopped lead now use `soft_stop` (below), rather than either measured braking rung. The corpus tops out at 25 m/s; above it the last rung holds.
- **The fast rung is what the driver's feet did; the car does not deliver it.** `MOONPILOT_FAST_ACCEL_V` is the p75 of the driver's *instantaneous* acceleration, but the planner hands it to the car as a closed-loop setpoint, and the plant does not land on it. Swept across all 434 cached segments (29 engaged events commanding ≥ 1.0 m/s² for ≥ 0.4 s at ≥ 2 m/s, peak `aEgo` searched 2 s past the run for the vehicle's own lag), the car delivers **1.27×** the ask below 3 m/s, 1.00× at 3–6, 1.07× at 6–10 and 1.13× at 10–15 — median 1.14× across all 29, min 0.91×, max 1.42×. Four separate routes show the same launch-peak shape (cmd 1.97 → `a` 2.43…2.51 at 2 m/s), so it is a plant characteristic and not one bad event. `MOONPILOT_FAST_ACCEL_TRACK_V` (0.786 at rest → 1.0 by 6 m/s, the inverse of the measured 0–3 m/s ratio) is applied to the fast rung only, so the ask is what the car actually gives while a merge at speed keeps the authority the driver measured. It is deliberately *not* `MoonpilotLongJerkScale`: that estimator is tighten-only, pinned at 1.000 over 362 paired ramps on this car, and caps the ramp into an acceleration rather than the plateau. Cost: a launch now asks ~21 % less than your feet at rest, which is the point; the ladder's own taper after 3.5 m/s is untouched.
- **The personality dial moves the ladder's error axis, never its rung magnitudes.** `MOONPILOT_ERR_BP_SCALE` — aggressive 0.7, standard 1.0, relaxed 1.4 — scales `MOONPILOT_CRUISE_ERR_BP` per frame's `selfdriveState.personality` (raw-value keyed, like `MOONPILOT_T_FOLLOW`): aggressive lands the driver's measured medium brake at −3.5 m/s of speed error instead of −5, relaxed needs −7, and the hold entry stays at 0 so the ladder keeps its shape — the same measured rungs binding earlier or later. The factors are hand-picked because the corpus cannot derive them: during manual driving `carState.vCruise` is valid on only ~5 % of frames (the stock ACC is off), so the driver's own error-to-rung response was never recorded. Nothing safety-side reads the axis — the lead's regulator, arrival and stopping floor, `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL`, `cruise_cap`, the jerk limit — and `forceDecel` short-circuits at `v_cruise` 0 before the interpolation. A re-measure over the corpus as it stands (265 of the original 308 segments survive on disk) corroborates the rungs rather than re-adopting anything: brake within 0.19 and coasting within 0.05 m/s² of the published values, the accel bands keeping their sign and order with the high-band deltas sitting where those missing segments' attrition is worst.
- **The vehicle's positive comfort jerk can tighten onroad, but never loosen safety braking.** `moonpilot/jerk.py` pairs the existing lag-aligned `a_target` command slope with `carState.aEgo` only while the fork owns longitudinal control, then slowly filters a ratio after 100 valid positive ramps. `MoonpilotLongJerkScale` persists the applied scale, defaults to 1.0, and is clamped to 0.5…1.0; `jerk_limit` applies it only to the positive comfort/launch ramp. The stopping-tested down ramp and `MOONPILOT_JERK_EMERGENCY` are untouched, and no estimate is exactly the nominal planner.

- **Cruise acceleration uses the runway before a slower lead.** For each present lead below set speed, the policy reserves the larger of the standstill gap and the requested headway, then estimates the relative distance needed to reach set speed at the current cruise ask and match the lead at `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL`. Below that distance, the cruise ask is capped at the measured slow accel rung; over one extra follow gap it blends back to the ordinary ladder. Current closing speed is part of that distance, so a slower lead tightens the cap even at the same range. This changes only positive cruise acceleration, and a clear road still uses the ordinary personality-scaled ladder. A lead accelerating by at least the existing fast-crawl threshold bypasses the cap; otherwise the estimate holds lead speed constant until the next planner tick.

- **The policy is closed-form, with one approach for every slower lead.** Three lead terms compete by `min`: the spacing regulator, relative-speed arrival and the stopping floor. The regulator holds `gap == max(stop_floor, t_follow * v_ego)` with its braking capped at `MOONPILOT_APPROACH_DECEL` (1.0 m/s²); `stop_floor` is `STOP_DISTANCE` at rest and grows with speed by up to `MOONPILOT_MOVING_STOP_RESERVE` (next-but-one bullet), a floor under headway, not an offset. Arrival targets `max(STOP_REST + reserve, t_follow * v_lead_arrive)`, continuously from a stopped lead to a moving one. The stopping floor keeps its own admission and can demand stronger braking. Cruise, curve, corridor and model candidates remain separate; the smallest wins, ties keeping the earlier candidate. The published trajectory rolls the same policy forward.
  On a closing approach, only the spacing regulator gets a soft inner cushion:
  `min(MOONPILOT_FOLLOW_CUSHION, max(v_ego - v_lead_match, 0) * K_V / K_GAP,
  max(t_follow * v_ego - stop_floor, 0))`. Below its 1 m cap that moving target exactly cancels
  the relative-speed brake at the nominal gap, so mild closing may cross it instead of treating the
  target as a wall. The cushion collapses with closing
  speed; once the speeds match, the speed floor (below) holds the command there, so the gap rests where
  the speeds met rather than being bought back with speed — a 0.196 m crossing at 20 m/s rests 0.165 m
  inside a 29 m target. The third bound tapers the cushion
  out before the time-gap target reaches the standstill floor. This never reaches the arrival or
  stopping-floor terms, so every stopping bound remain
  intact.
- **The lead slot coasts in measured low-speed stop-and-go, then yields to safety.** Across six clean
  manual launch→brake cycles in the route corpus, all six ego speeds were 1.58–3.87 m/s and the driver
  coasted before braking in every case; coast occupied 85 % of the first 1.5 s after the detected lead
  deceleration, against 8 % braking and 7 % gas. The follow cushion itself is already zero below
  `STOP_DISTANCE / t_follow` (4.1 m/s at standard personality), so this cap remains separate inside
  the lead slot: the ego must be closing at at most 4.0 m/s, whether the lead is moving or stopped, with the gap
  at most 10 m, and the estimated lead acceleration at most −0.3 m/s². It takes the measured
  level-road coast value for the ego speed (−0.10 m/s² up to 3.5 m/s, easing to −0.125 at 4.0).
  **Closing is the cue, not the lead's braking alone**: a braking lead that is still pulling away
  opens the gap, and capping there braked a launching car behind it and, as the opening gap crossed
  the 10 m edge, turned into an accelerate/brake/accelerate wave. While closing, the gap only shrinks,
  so that edge is crossed inward once. `policy` applies the cap with `min` **after** `lead_accel`, so the arrival
  and stopping-floor candidates still win whenever the physical gap requires more braking. When the
  lead's estimated acceleration leaves that band, the cap disappears on the next frame and the ordinary
  spacing/cruise candidates recover follow distance; there is no stored cushion or launch state. The
  speed and gap ceilings are deliberate: the corpus has no engaged high-speed stop-and-go evidence, and
  high-speed coasts cross the approach bounds described below.
- **An oversized gap never raises the cruise slot.** At or above the set speed, the ordinary
  speed-error term is non-positive and remains the ceiling even when the spacing regulator wants to
  close a larger gap. A transient brake can therefore leave the car farther back behind a lead
  holding the set speed; recovery waits for the lead to pull away or the ego to fall below set speed
  rather than buying follow-distance recovery with extra acceleration or overspeed.
- **The model's braking ask passes through outside experimental mode, gated only by a deadband.** `MoonpilotModelBraking` (on by default) admits `modelV2.action.desiredAcceleration` as a fourth candidate in every mode, but only once it asks for at least `MOONPILOT_MODEL_BRAKE_THRESHOLD` of braking — without that deadband `min` turns model dither into a one-way ratchet against the cruise term and the car settles below the set speed on a clear road. Past it the ask blends in from the other candidates' minimum over `MOONPILOT_MODEL_BRAKE_FADE_T` (0.5 s), proportionally faster the deeper it is and at once from `MOONPILOT_MODEL_BRAKE_FULL` (−2.0), and then goes in whole, so a deep model brake reaches full authority on `MOONPILOT_JERK_EMERGENCY`'s ramp; the published rollout projects the same fade timer forward per sample rather than holding the current weight. The blend replaced a step: on route `00000408` at 15 m/s an ask admitted at −0.50 moved the plan −0.23 → −0.52 in 0.15 s and the car answered −0.84; replayed, the same entry takes ~0.4 s and reaches the same −0.58. Fading *above* the deadband instead was rejected: 13 % of that route's clear-road engaged frames sit between −0.2 and −0.5, which is the ratchet. What bounds it is `ACCEL_MIN`, and that bound is not a fork value: the clip every command passes through in `update`, the identical clip in the published rollout, and the controller's own `accel_limits` all enforce it, and nothing bypasses them — `controlsState.forceDecel` is driver-monitoring escalation, not an emergency override. Because `policy` takes the minimum, the candidate can only ever add braking, so the fork's "at least as much braking as the kinematic law" argument is untouched. The model's `action.shouldStop` deliberately stays experimental-only: it is `v_ego < 0.3 and a < 0.1`, the creep state the should-stop trailing gate exists to exclude. A fork-owned floor *above* `ACCEL_MIN` was tried and removed: it withheld braking the driver had already begun, and its `meta.hardBrakePredicted` release valve never fired.
- **A stop the model began is a stop the model finishes.** The deadband is hysteresis once the model holds the slot: it keeps it until its ask rises past `model_release(v_ego)`, which is the entry deadband at and above `MOONPILOT_MODEL_STOP_SPEED` (5 m/s) and climbs to `should_stop`'s own 0.1 at creep speed. On route `000003d2` the model braked a clear-road stop from 17 m/s and its tapering ask crossed back over −0.5 at 0.9 m/s; the cruise term took the slot, asked +1.0, and was moving the car again when the driver braked. In the crawl band the rule is now exactly experimental mode's stop contract — held while the model's ask is under 0.1 — so the car also launches when the model asks to go. That is the ceiling: the model, not a lead, decides when a lead-less stop ends. Over that route's eight lead-less standstills the ask crossed 0.1 between 0.3 and 5.4 s before the driver moved off on six and not at all on two (the car would have held), and on two long parked waits (73 s, 12 min) it crossed a minute or more early — the driver is the backstop there, as in experimental mode. At speed nothing changed, so there is still no ratchet below the set speed.
- **A departing lead lifts that hold once the model asks to go at all.** The hold is for stops the model owns; behind a lead it outlived its purpose. On route `00000414` the model braked the approach to a stopped queue, kept the slot through 20 s at standstill, and when the lead pulled away its ask rose only to +0.04…+0.09 — under the 0.1 release — so the plan sat there and the stop hold stayed on for 1.8 s while the gap opened 5.2 → 6.3 m, until the driver pressed the gas. While `radarState.leadOne` is present and moving faster than `MOONPILOT_SHOULD_STOP_SPEED` and the model's ask is positive, the release is the entry deadband instead, so the slot goes back to the lead terms and their departure crawl. Replayed on that route the plan crosses 0.1 within 0.1 s of the lead moving and holds +0.31. A model still asking ≤ 0 keeps the hold, so a lead driving off through a light the model is braking for does not take the car with it; with no lead present the hold is unchanged.
- **Stopped and moving leads share relative-speed arrival.** `lead_accel` evaluates `arrival_accel(closing, gap - target, a_lead)` whenever braking-previewed closing is positive, with `target = max(rest point, t_follow * v_lead_arrive)` (rest point: next bullet) and `v_lead_arrive = max(0, v_lead + min(a_comfort, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T)` — `a_comfort` being the lead's accel faded out below `MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_COMFORT_NOISE` (see the coast-cushion bullet); the stopping floor uses the raw `v_lead_eff = max(0, v_lead + min(a_lead, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T)`. There is no separate stopped-lead roll-in, nor a gate at 0.1 m/s or `STOP_DISTANCE / t_follow`: a lead slowing to rest changes the same target continuously. Positive lead acceleration is credited only to the comfort arrival and tracking terms, not to the stopping floor.
- **A slow moving lead is coasted toward first, then braked for, and keeps a soft-stop reserve that melts into the stop.** A far slow lead used to be braked like a stopped one from first sight: 12 → 3 m/s from 80 m crossed −0.25 m/s² at 77.8 m (pre-change commit `99f9a211f`). Arrival is now floored at the coast (the brake band's −0.10) while the braking budget at `MOONPILOT_ARRIVAL_COAST_DECEL` (0.8 m/s²) leaves more than `MOONPILOT_ARRIVAL_COAST_T` (1.5 s) of closing, blending back to the original law over that last 1.5 s and below 1.2 m/s of closing; the budget reads `stopping_decel` on the raw, sustain-bounded lead braking, so a slowing lead spends it. Behind a moving lead both low-speed targets — arrival's rest point from `STOP_REST`, the regulator's floor from `STOP_DISTANCE` — rise to `STOP_DISTANCE + MOONPILOT_MOVING_STOP_RESERVE` (8 m), and the coast applies, each scaled by `min(v / MOONPILOT_CREEP_SPEED, 1)` on the lead's braking-previewed speed (the regulator's on the slower car): the reserve melts linearly into a stop and a stopped lead's approach is bit-identical. The at-rest floor stays `STOP_DISTANCE`, so the car starts no further back, but a launch lets the gap open toward the reserve (behind a 1 m/s² lead, 0.435 at 0.25 s against 0.478). `python -m moonpilot.lab lead-approach --smoke-only` (`_fly`, point-mass): that approach first asks past −0.25 at 65.7 m, peaks −0.72 (−0.61 pre-change) and settles 7.06 m back (5.07); behind 1.5 m/s 6.55 m; if the lead stops at −1.5, −1.33 (−1.17), resting 4.89 m; a stopped lead and the 16 → 11.7 m/s coast-to-target are unchanged. A 1.0 budget moved the onset to 54.8 m at −0.95 / −1.54; 0.7 to 74.2 m; 0.6 never coasts. The settle sits ~0.9–1.1 m inside the floor (`MOONPILOT_ARRIVAL_MIN_T` floors arrival's slack at 1.5 s of closing, inside which it asks only `-closing / 3`; once speeds match the follow speed floor holds the gap), more behind faster leads (15 → 5 m/s: 5.71 m against a 7.25 m time gap, 5.69 pre-change). Manual (same command): braked stationary median 4.7 m (n = 77); speed-matched ≈3.7 m below 1 m/s (n = 16), ≈6.5 m at 1–2 m/s (n = 5) — unpaired windows, compatible with this, not a measurement. **Low-speed follow does not flicker from this.** Below 5 m/s neither brake band nor coast cushion acts; behind a steady 1.5 or 3 m/s lead with σ 0.03 m/s radar speed noise the command dithers within ±0.1 m/s² (never past ±0.2) with or without the reserve. In a noisy 0 ↔ 2.5 m/s stop-and-go, ±0.05 reversals fell 61 → 45 per 2 min, peak −1.39 → −0.68. To watch: the follow speed floor's hard `v_ego > MOONPILOT_CREEP_SPEED` gate steps the command by `K_GAP` × any matched-speed deficit at creep, and the reserve widens that window from under 6 m to under 8 m; recorded drives hold ~6 s of engaged slow following, too little to test it. The stopping floor, the crawl cap, FCW and every safety gate are untouched; a turning lead is still a lead until radard drops it.
- **Catch-up fades out before stop-and-go.** Below set speed and while the lead is not braking (`a_lead > -MOONPILOT_LEAD_ACCEL_TAU_RESET`), arrival can earn the existing slow-rung credit while the gentle coast still has room to match. That credit is weighted continuously from 0 when `t_follow * v_lead_eff` equals `MOONPILOT_STOP_REST` to 1 at the larger of `MOONPILOT_STOP_DISTANCE` and the moving rest target, where the former moving-lead catch-up began. A crawling or stopped target therefore gets no catch-up throttle, without a binary moving/stopped switch. The cruise candidate remains a ceiling and the no-throttle coast still includes grade when computing the catch-up room.
- **The rest point is a soft aim, not a final-meter precision chase.** Arrival floors its slack at `MOONPILOT_ARRIVAL_MIN_T * closing` (1.5 s), so its braking falls with closing even after the preferred 5.2 m rest point is crossed. The separate `-v_ego² / (2 * max(gap - STOP_REST, 0.1))` crawl finish is removed. At the reproduced state of 0.68 m/s and 5.2 m gap, the raw lead ask is now approximately −0.65 m/s² instead of −2.31. The spacing regulator still brakes a gap deficit; this is not permission to drive through the lead.
- **A stop behind a stopped lead gets a softer final ask without chasing an exact gap.** Below `MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_V` (fully under 3 m/s, out by 5) and behind a lead whose braking-credited speed is under `MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_LEAD_V`, `soft_stop` replaces the spacing regulator's brake with the kinematic stop toward `MOONPILOT_STOP_REST + MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_REST_PER_V * v` (5.2 m + 0.15 s * speed). The moving rest point and shrinking `v²` ease the ask toward rest. Slack is floored at `MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_MIN_T * v` (0.5 s), so crossing the preferred rest point while rolling does not produce a near-zero-distance brake spike. This is only a comfort law: its weight is zero at physical gaps at or below `MOONPILOT_MIN_SLACK` (1 m), rises linearly over the next meter, and is fully available from 2 m. Closer leads retain the original spacing brake; a time-only floor otherwise drove a 0.68 m/s crawl through a stopped lead initially 0.5 m away, even on the instantaneous test plant. The short-gap regression restores the old regulator's 0.143 m remaining clearance in that case and 0.443 m from an initial 0.8 m gap; it does not claim every initially unsafe approach can be recovered. Positive asks remain untouched; the last `MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_HANDOFF_V` (0.1 m/s) of closing hands back to the regulator for the existing stop hold and launch behavior. Route `0000040e`'s 126–138 s queue stop held −0.54…−0.77 m/s² down to 0.3 m/s; replay now asks −0.19 instead of −0.54 at 0.30 m/s (re-run: `python -m moonpilot.lab queue-stop` reads −0.190 against the logged −0.537 at 0.297 m/s in that window). On the point-mass test plant, normal stops from 5–12 m/s rest roughly 4.9–5.2 m back, while late detections and braking leads rest roughly 4.6–4.7 m back. The final planner asks ease to roughly −0.1…−0.2 m/s² without increasing those scenarios' peak braking. These are planner/plant results, not measurements of the changed car: `longcontrol` retains its 0.2 m/s² minimum rolling-stop deceleration, and physical stopping distance and comfort still require a drive. The logged standstill spikes in `aEgo` alone are not evidence of a brake grab: the wheel-speed cutoff and speed-filter overshoot occur in both stops; Toyota's `VSC1S07.GVC` and the device IMU distinguish the real lead-stop jolt from that artifact.
- **The stopping floor carries the stopping margin, and blends in.** Its slack to the rest anchor (1 m minimum) holds back `MOONPILOT_STOP_LAG_T` (0.15 s) of closing: a constant-decel stop owes the closing it did not see at its end, a margin a since-removed time-to-collision term bought by braking harder mid-approach. Admission keeps the 1.0…1.6 m/s² speed schedule, blended over `MOONPILOT_FLOOR_ADMISSION_BAND` (0.3 m/s²) below it: past it `min(a, a_stop)` as before, below it only partial and only deeper. A hard switch flipped a 12 m/s stop's crawl tail between −1.0 and −1.34 from 75–80 m starts (`test_a_crawl_stop_tail_does_not_redeepen_as_the_floor_admits`). Stopped-lead rest gaps at 90 / 118.8 / 129.6 / 144 kph: 4.92 / 4.68 / 4.54 / 4.28 m with the term, 4.99 / 4.90 / 4.88 / 4.58 m with this (3.98 m at 144 with neither; 4.15 once the comfort cap below spends it); the 144 kph peak 2.50 → 2.14 m/s². `python -m moonpilot.lab hard-brake` (a lead braking to rest from a 1.45 s follow, both cars on the trapezoid), with the sustain below: every −3.5 case clears (≥ 3.6 m, ≥ 4.8 m lagged); −5 contacts at 30 m/s and at every lagged speed, upstream's planner in 11 of 12. FCW and `ACCEL_MIN` are unchanged. Upstream's maneuver suite does not reach those speeds; the fork's closed-loop tests do, with lead range pipeline-limited near 131.9 m (radar: 228 m).
- **Radar latency remains part of the lead's age.** Toyota's `dRel` is older than the `carState` radard uses to compose `vLead`, even where `CP.radarDelay` is zero. `moonpilot/radar_latency.py` learns that residual from `vLead / aEgo` for stationary radar targets, requiring `|aEgo| ≥ 0.6`, a ratio in (0, 0.3] s, and 50 gated samples before applying it. `_lead_age` adds that value only for radar leads; `MoonpilotRadarLatency` and `MoonpilotRadarLatencySamples` persist the estimate and its evidence. The stopping-floor anchor still tapers from 6 m toward the 5.2 m soft rest target below 4 m/s. Re-run today, `python -m moonpilot.lab radar-latency` on the ten engaged local drives reads 129.7–132.8 ms applied, within 0.4 ms of each drive's logged `MoonpilotRadarLatency`. It is a bounded aim, not a promise to finish on an exact centimeter; closed-loop regressions retain at least the comfort cap's one-meter allowance below `MOONPILOT_CRAWL_REST`.
- **A lead that pulls away lifts the stop hold at once, from inside the standstill gap.** Every stop finishes inside the 6 m standstill gap (the rest point is 5.2 m; the car rests nearer 4.5 m on the road), so at the launch the regulator's spacing error is a brake — −0.44 m/s² from 4.5 m — and on route `000003d2` it kept the hold on for a second after the lead moved, until the driver took over with the gas. `departure_crawl` floors the regulator, while the lead is moving and faster than the ego, at its relative-speed term alone capped at `MOONPILOT_FAST_CRAWL`, fading over the crawl band like `crawl_accel`; the lead's speed carries its accel both ways, so a braking lead is owed nothing, and speed-matched the floor is zero — the gap is kept at a crawl, not recovered, and every braking term still reads the physical gap. With the car parked, a negative plan target is also no longer a jerk baseline (the hold owns the brake), so the release is two frames after the lead's speed clears the rest deadband. Replayed against that launch: the hold lifts ~0.5 s sooner, the car crawls at +0.31, and the driver's fast-accel ladder then takes over (+1.70 within 2 s when replayed under the former flat 1.7 budget; the launch band now allows the ladder's own ~1.97 peak).

- **The floor is measured in the frame the gap actually closes in, and it is where the fork's worst braking defect lived.** `stopping_decel(v_ego, v_lead, a_lead, slack, sustain)` replaces `-(v_ego² - v_lead²) / (2·slack)`, which is exact only for a *stopped* lead: while the lead keeps travelling the gap closes at the relative rate, so what spends the slack is `(v_ego - v_lead)² / 2a`, and the absolute form was deeper than the geometry by `(v_ego + v_lead) / (v_ego - v_lead)` for every moving one. On the device's route `000003c8` that was 0.82 m/s² deeper than the geometry on 45 of 45 engaged braking ticks (a 4.4–5.3× over-ask), and because the regulator's output is positive at those gaps and the TTC term is not yet binding, the floor was the *only* admitted braking candidate — it governed by default, and the command sat flat on it: **a −1.0…−1.17 m/s² plateau held ~8 s behind a lead 70–115 m away**, which is what a driver reports as "braking too strong too early, like a decel floor rather than a ramp". The positive surge after it was the cruise slot refilling the speed error the phantom brake dug, not a second defect: post-brake recovery peaks are unchanged by the fix (max +1.60 either way). What ships now is one continuous law in two exact pieces, verified against a brute-force minimum-constant-decel solver (1 ms integration, bisection) to ≤0.002 m/s² on states spanning both:
  - the **late crossing**, where the ego is still faster when the lead's braking ends: `-(v_ego - v_end)² / (2·(slack + 0.5·|a_lead|·t²))`, with `t = min(sustain, time to the lead's rest)` and `v_end` the speed that leaves it. The lead's travel beyond a lead permanently at `v_end` cancels analytically and leaves that `0.5·|a_lead|·t²` of extra room.
  - the **early crossing**, `2·slack <= closing · t`, where the ego matches the lead's speed while the lead is still braking. Crediting the whole braking-phase travel there credits travel the lead has not made: at `v_ego` 25 behind a lead doing 10 and braking at −0.5 with 25 m of slack the late form reads −2.50 where the truth is −5.00, because the ego comes to rest at 10 s having been given the lead's full 20 s. The relative speeds converge at `a - |a_lead|`, so the requirement is `|a_lead| + closing² / (2·slack)`. The two pieces agree exactly at the boundary, so this is a continuous law rather than a blend.

  **The sustain horizon is the dial, sized from both ends** and flown closed-loop (contact = gap < 0.4 m): `max(MOONPILOT_LEAD_BRAKE_SUSTAIN_T, MOONPILOT_LEAD_BRAKE_SUSTAIN_PER_A · |a_lead|)` of the estimator's measured accel (`brake_sustain`), 2.0 s up to −2 m/s² and longer for a harder brake. No prediction (`sustain` 0) contacts a lead braking −3.5 or −5 to rest from the 1.45 s follow at 20 m/s, and a 15 m cut-in that brakes at −4. Predicting the lead to rest is safe but turns a lead *tapping* −5 for 0.5 s at 30 m/s from 60 m into −3.50 m/s² for an end gap of ~40 m either way. A fixed 2.0 s held that tap to −0.82 but contacted behind a lead braking −3.5 to rest from 30 m/s (−0.18 m) and at −5 from 25 m/s; scaled, both clear (3.73 and 0.96 m; 4.95 m against −5.95 lagged; `python -m moonpilot.lab sustain`, both cars on the trapezoid). The cost is the hard tap, −2.96; a −2 tap still peaks at the approach decel. Over 12 engaged drives (replay vs logged p95 0.06–0.66: they ran older code) it deepens 92 of 55k frames by > 0.05, one −1.02 on `00000408`. −5 on the lagged plant contacts at every horizon, and a lead braking at −8 contacts at every horizon including infinity: the ego needs more than `ACCEL_MIN` there, which is physics and not this constant.

  **FCW keeps the unbounded horizon** — `required_decel` passes `sustain=inf`, so it predicts the lead to rest. The two differ deliberately: the planner's bound exists so a moderate brake tap does not buy a stop, and a warning behind its own −4 m/s² gate has no comfort to protect, so the only thing a shorter horizon can do there is stay silent through a real one (at `v_ego` 30 behind a lead doing 28 at 25 m braking at −6, to-rest reads −5.00 and fires where a fixed 2 s horizon read −2.67, and that contact is genuinely unavoidable). The relative frame reaches FCW too, and removes a false-positive class: a lead holding 28 m/s 12 m ahead of a car doing 30 reads −0.17 where the absolute form read −4.94 and fired on an ordinary overtake.

  Measured on route `000003c8` against the pre-fix law: the engaged block's plateau went to **0.00 s** with **zero new braking ticks at any threshold**, and only states with `a_lead <= -1.0` got deeper. The early-crossing piece is selected on 22 % of the planner arm's `a_lead`-live states and 29 % of FCW's.
- **A lead's acceleration is anticipated one-sidedly, and only its braking half reaches the safety terms.** `v_lead_match = v_lead + max(a_comfort, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` (`a_comfort`: the accel faded out inside its noise, see the coast-cushion bullet) is the speed the spacing regulator matches — the lead's *next* speed — and it never reaches the safety terms: a credit there would release braking on a predicted launch, which is braking for a prediction where the geometry had not moved. The braking direction does reach them, deliberately: `v_lead_eff = v_lead + min(a_lead, 0) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` is the speed `closing` and the floor are measured against, so a lead that is braking is matched as if it had already shed `MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T` of that braking — half a second, and the length is the whole trade. It is no longer the *only* braking prediction: `stopping_decel` makes its own, 2 s or longer (and unbounded on the FCW arm), on top of this credited speed — the two stack, which is why the sustain bound had to be sized with this constant's own tap scenario in hand. (The estimator's accel reaches both terms one further way — `update` hands the policy `lead_state_at`'s speed at `action_t + lead_age`, ~0.25 s of it, which is the delay match the bullet below describes.) This credit buys the onset and it buys the stopping margin on a *sustained* brake, which is the part that decided its length. What the credit costs is in the constant's own comment — the regulator's equilibrium moves `(K_V / K_GAP) * MOONPILOT_LEAD_PREVIEW_T * a_lead` closer, 1 m per m/s² of lead accel, held only while the estimate says the lead is still gaining. Measured: a settled follow behind a lead holding 0.5 m/s² sits 0.35 m closer than the setpoint where the uncredited law sits 0.13 m behind it, and a lead that launches at 1 m/s² for 3 s then brakes at −3 to rest ends 6 cm *further* back — the transient does not survive the approach. Against a hard launch it is worth little alone — a 2 m/s² lead puts the ask above the comfort ramp, so 0.45 m/s² at 0.25 s is all either arm delivers and the credit's own contribution is 0.005 m of gap by 2 s — while a slow lead needs no help: a 0.5 m/s² launch draws 0.292 m/s² at 0.25 s against 0.165, and 0.15 m of gap by 2 s. On top of the launch jerk the two together reach 1.075 m/s² at 0.25 s against 0.672, and 0.42 m of gap by 2 s. The channel is real but not the only one: `lead_state_at`'s projection below still carries the same accel into the *gap*, which is why a launch answers at all when the jerk limit is the binding constraint. `test_the_lead_accel_credit_splits_by_direction_and_by_term` pins both halves by arithmetic at states where each governs, `test_a_launch_is_answered_through_the_projection_and_the_regulator` the two positive channels, and `test_noise_is_averaged_not_amplified` the estimator that feeds them. Measured closed-loop beside that equilibrium: stop-and-go launches from the 6 m standstill target with the lead gaining at 1/2/3 m/s² hold minimum live-target errors of −0.280/0/0 m with the gap floor at 6.0, the reverse closing case (ego 15, lead 8, 28 m gap) shows a largest inward error of 0.222 m, and settled follows at 0.5/1.0 m/s² sit −0.30/−0.33 m recovering in 1.4/0.1 s — every taper tried increased return overshoot or weakened the pinned launch response, so no positive-side taper ships.
- **The up-jerk is scheduled on speed, and the first step of a ramp is the comfort step.** `jerk_limit` reads `MOONPILOT_JERK_LAUNCH` at and below `MOONPILOT_JERK_LAUNCH_SPEED`, tapering to `MOONPILOT_JERK_UP` by twice it — but only once `a_prev` is off zero: 0.075 m/s² cannot cross `should_stop`'s 0.1 release threshold in one frame and 0.2 can, so the first-step rule is what keeps a parked car's brake hold out of a single frame of a lead's reported speed, which is the same property the `trailing` gate protects from the state machine's side (`test_the_stop_gate_follows_the_governing_candidate_not_every_lead`). Measured closed-loop from rest behind a lead pulling away at 2 m/s²: 0.5 m/s² at 0.15 s against 0.30 s on the comfort ramp, and 0.14 m of the gap by 2 s. In that case it is also what makes the anticipation bullet above pay: the credit alone is worth 0.005 m there (the ask is above this ramp either way, so 0.45 m/s² at 0.25 s is all either arm delivers), while the two together are worth 0.56 m, with the ask at 1.075 m/s² by 0.25 s — where a *slow* lead needs no such help, drawing 0.292 m/s² against 0.165 with the credit alone. Scheduling on the speed rather than on a state is what lets the published rollout — which carries its own speed — roll forward under the same rule the command was taken from. `test_the_up_jerk_is_scheduled_on_speed_and_the_down_jerk_is_not` pins the schedule, the first-step rule and the down side's onset below.
- **A shallow brake onsets at the driver's pace; an urgent one does not.** The down-jerk reads the ask: `MOONPILOT_JERK_ONSET` (1.0 m/s³) at and above `MOONPILOT_JERK_ONSET_A` (−1.0 m/s²), `MOONPILOT_JERK_DOWN` (2.0) at −2.0, `MOONPILOT_JERK_EMERGENCY` (10.0) at `ACCEL_MIN`. Route `00000414` stepped two shallow asks — a new slow lead's arrival term at −0.88, model braking at −1.0 — onto the 2.0 ramp, and the Toyota PCM's own compensation overshot it (`aEgo` −1.31 against −0.99 commanded), while the driver's own onsets on routes `00000408`–`00000414` measured 0.4–0.5 m/s³ median over 196 manual brake applications. The gentle onset is off in two places where a shallow ask is the start of something urgent: below `MOONPILOT_JERK_LAUNCH_SPEED`, fading in by twice it, because at a crawl a close stopped lead makes any ask urgent (0.5 m at 0.68 m/s closed to 4 mm at 1.0), and while any lead is slowing past `MOONPILOT_JERK_ONSET_LEAD_A` (−0.2 m/s²), because a lead braking at −3.5 m/s² first draws a shallow ask only because its braking just began — with that gate the command reaches −0.5 / −1.0 at 0.30 / 0.55 s exactly as at 2.0, where −0.5 cost 0.05 s and −0.1 sat inside the estimator's slope noise. Replayed through the planner on that route, the shallow onsets reach −0.5 m/s² 0.05–0.25 s later; asks at −2.0 or deeper are unchanged by it, and the crawl stop at 448 s is the next bullet's.
- **A stop spends the rest gap before it brakes past the driver.** Below `MOONPILOT_COMFORT_STOP_V` (whole under 12 m/s, out by 16) `lead_accel`'s braking never exceeds the larger of a comfort decel — `MOONPILOT_CRAWL_STOP_DECEL` (1.5 m/s², the driver's crawl-stop peak) at a crawl, `MOONPILOT_COMFORT_STOP_DECEL` (2.0) from 5 m/s — and the `stopping_decel` that rests one `MOONPILOT_MIN_SLACK` short of `MOONPILOT_CRAWL_REST` (4 m); lighter asks are untouched. On route `00000414` at 448 s the car crawled at 3.1 m/s 8.2 m behind a lead that stopped; every lead term aimed 5.2–6 m back and the car pulled −2.77 m/s². The driver's own 13 stops in that geometry (`00000408`–`00000414`) peaked −1.31…−1.90 and rested 2.7–5.9 m back. Flown from that state against a lead braking at −1.0 / −1.5 / −2.5 m/s², the old law peaked −1.62 / −1.93 / −2.02; today's planner −1.17 / −1.50 / −1.50, resting 5.04 / 4.91 / 4.62 m back. Aiming at 4 m exactly landed 3.72 m (2.96 m from 4.5 m/s at a stopped lead 9 m away); the extra meter is that margin. Inside `MOONPILOT_CRAWL_REST + MOONPILOT_MIN_SLACK` the cap's own ask deepens without bound, so the close-gap brake is untouched. Replaying the logged 448 s inputs, the command peaks −2.16 against −2.50 (`python -m moonpilot.lab crawl-stop --routes 00000414`: −2.156 against the logged −2.111) — open-loop, on a trajectory the old command already shaped. It runs to 16 m/s because of route `00000451` at 175 s: at 7 m/s a lead braking −3.5 14 m ahead drew −2.88 to rest 5 m back; capped, −2.44 open-loop, with the hard-brake margins above 0.3–0.5 m smaller and no new contact. A stop from speed behind a stopped lead rests nearer 4.4 m than 4.7, so the soft stop's standstill handoff (`MOONPILOT_SOFT_STOP_HANDOFF_V`) returns the regulator's hold 0.05–0.07 m/s² deeper than the easing plan at 0.1 m/s, where `stopping` already holds at least 0.2.
- **The crawl band.** With no pedal the car settles at idle creep, a median 1.9 m/s (`MOONPILOT_CREEP_SPEED`) on level road, and climbs to it at +0.31 (`MOONPILOT_FAST_CRAWL`) from 0.5–1 m/s, which caps the regulator's creep (`crawl_accel`). The two braking-side crawl rungs are measured and deliberately not applied, because the regulator already stops at the driver's rate: below creep speed the driver brakes (83 % of frames) at a median −0.08 around 0.5 m/s, and over 212 stops averages −0.47 in the last second (p25 −0.81, p75 −0.26) and −0.37 in the two before. The unshaped regulator, from 5–25 m/s at a stopped lead, read −0.31 and −0.47 over the same windows and rested 5.19 m back — inside the driver's spread, with a softer finish rather than a firmer one; compressing its braking toward the rungs made that finish softer still (−0.28) and rested 4.63 m back.

  **Grade coasting is a separate no-lead policy.** `MoonpilotCoastGrade` reads the live vehicle pitch already carried in `carControl.orientationNED[1]` and applies inside the cruise candidate, not in `longcontrol.py`: the controller still tracks one scalar net-acceleration target and therefore handles gravity as a plant disturbance. The fitted `coast_accel` is the no-throttle target; when its road term exceeds `MOONPILOT_COAST_GRADE_MIN` (0.4 m/s², about a 7% grade) and pushes the car away from the set speed, the target tapers toward that coast value inside `MOONPILOT_COAST_BAND` (1.5 m/s, 3.4 mph / 5.4 km/h). Moderate roads stay on exact set-speed control; a high-grade descent can run above the set speed instead of braking immediately, and a high-grade climb can bleed speed instead of adding throttle when throttle is otherwise permitted. Recovery from that sag is not the ladder: just past the climb band edge, in `[band, 2*band)` of underspeed, the positive ask is capped at `MOONPILOT_COAST_RECOVERY_ACCEL` (0.15 m/s²) so it pulls back slow and steady on the same hill — deeper deficits and every other grade keep the plain law, so a launch is unaffected. The descent branch still applies when the model's `allow_throttle` gate is false, because that existing cap never relaxes braking above the set speed; the no-throttle climb path already coasts and remains unchanged. Missing pose, level/sub-threshold grade, feature-off and experimental mode are pass-throughs. Lead, stopping-floor, curve and model-braking candidates still win through `min`, and the existing jerk limiter smooths the transition. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins both signs, the band edge, the recovery cap and its window, the allow-throttle gates, the closed-loop bounds and the published rollout; its upstream graded maneuvers remain green.
- **The pitch every grade term reads carries a standing offset, and it is learned out.** `carControl.orientationNED[1]` is locationd's calibrated pose and the fork's only grade input, so whatever bias it carries is a hill the planner believes in forever. That bias is measured rather than suspected, on two routes now: the reported pitch is negative on **0.23 %** of 136,440 `carControl` frames with a **+2.27°** median on a 23-segment route, and on **0.95 %** of 7,913 gated frames with a **+2.06°** median (mean 0.0353 rad, per-segment medians 1.47–2.38°) on this 14-segment route — positive and multi-degree on this device with the floor never dropping out, so a standing bias rather than terrain. On the 23-segment route, removing its `carState.aEgo` correlation (r = 0.145, so not body pitch under acceleration) leaves +2.29°, and the drive **ends 71 m from where it started** — a GPS fix at both ends — while integrating `sin(pitch)·ds` over the same 7.93 km claims **+298 m of climb**. That is `asin(298/7927)` = **2.16°**, which `coast_accel`'s road term reads as **−0.21 m/s² of permanent uphill**; its cost is the descent branch, since `_coast_applies` needs a negative pitch and a real descent therefore has to exceed ~6.3° before the band can open at all (Toyota's own `sin(min(pitch, 0))·g` is clamped to zero for the same reason). `moonpilot/pitch.py` learns it as the long-window mean of the pitch while the car is driving — `MOONPILOT_PITCH_RC` 600 s, above `MOONPILOT_PITCH_MIN_SPEED`, trusted after `MOONPILOT_PITCH_MIN_SAMPLES` — and the planner subtracts `applied()` before `coast_accel` sees it. Four things about it. It learns **whenever the car is driving, not only while engaged**, because the bias belongs to the device and the car rather than to who is steering, which is exactly why it is measurable where `moonpilot/latency.py`'s estimate is not: the same corpus holds ~2.4 min of engaged longitudinal control against 136k pose frames. Its **bound is sized above the bias, not against the gate** — `MOONPILOT_PITCH_OFFSET_MAX` is 3°, over the measured 2.16°, because a clamp that binds on a real device saturates silently and under-corrects forever, and `moonpilot/tests/test_pitch.py` pins that ordering so the tempting fix of tightening it is refused. The ceiling on the other side is named rather than argued away: a *wrongly* saturated offset is `sin(3°)·5.65` = 0.296 m/s² of road term, which does open the coast band on level road wherever `MOONPILOT_COAST_GRADE_MIN` sits below that, and what bounds the overspeed there is `MOONPILOT_COAST_BAND`. Reaching it takes a window that is one long climb. It **persists in `MoonpilotPitchOffset`** because the window is longer than most drives: replayed through that route at plannerd's cadence it crosses `estimated` at 300 s and reaches 1.30° of the 2.16° truth over the route's 555 s of driving (the routes behind that trajectory are not in this checkout; `python -m moonpilot.lab pitch` replays the ten engaged local drives from each one's own persisted seed and reaches 2.09–2.27° applied, within 0.26° of the logged `MoonpilotPitchOffset`), which is `1 − exp(−555/600)` — under-converged, therefore under-corrected, which is the conservative direction, and it finishes across drives rather than restarting at zero each boot. From a zero start on this 14-segment route the same shape repeats exactly: the replay lands at 0.0170 rad, which is `mean×(1−e^(−396/600))` — the RC=600 s filter behaving as designed — while the persisted `MoonpilotPitchOffset` is 0.0159 rad (0.91°), under half this route's 0.0353 rad mean, leaving ~0.19 m/s² of phantom uphill in `coast_accel`; but the value is young, not stuck: the pitch feature reached the device hours before this drive, the param mtime falls inside the drive span, and a zero-start replay reproduces the persisted 0.0159 to four decimals — a first full drive from a zero start, so the RC must not be shortened on this evidence; re-check after ~30 min of accumulated valid driving, which should read ≥ 0.030. And the assumption is that **routes come back down**: a window that is one long climb biases it, bounded by the clamp. No row in **The registry** — a learned value is not a behavior the driver flips. End to end on a genuine 5° descent reported through that bias, the planner brakes at −0.625 m/s² uncorrected and coasts at +0.093 with the offset learned, against +0.0938 if the pitch had been truthful.
- **The two inputs are not the same age, and they were being projected as if they were.** `action_t` carries the actuator delay and one model period, applied to the ego and the leads alike — but `radarState` lands about a full cycle behind the `modelV2` tick that polls it. Measured from the logs over 22.8k planner ticks on 25 routes: median age at the tick is **46.2 ms for the lead against 4.1 ms for `carState`**, and radar is the staler input on **100 %** of ticks there (lead-lag median 41 ms, p95 46 ms, never negative). On this 14-segment route the median agrees within 1.2 ms — 47.4 ms over 16,058 ticks — but the positive fraction reads 99.98 %: 3 negative ticks to −2.9 ms, at segment boundaries. So on every tick the planner compared a fresh ego against a gap from ~46 ms ago, and the closing distance in that interval was simply lost. `update` now ages the lead by exactly `modelV2.logMonoTime - radarState.logMonoTime` — from the stamps the SubMaster already carries, so no new constant and nothing to calibrate — and applies both halves of that interval: the lead's own travel rides on the extra prediction time (`action_t + lead_age`), the ego's on `x_stale = v_ego * lead_age`. The published rollout takes the same correction, so the plan is the prediction the command was taken from rather than a fresher one. It cannot cost safety *where the ego can close* at the age that actually occurs, but that is now a measurement rather than a structural guarantee. The old proof was structural because the old floor ignored `a_lead` entirely; `stopping_decel` reads it, and `lead_state_at` decays it, so an aged lead could read as braking less and therefore needing less. So the age adds travel but not decay: `update` and the rollout decay the lead's accel over `action_t` only (`t_decay`) and take the sustain horizon from the measured accel (`brake_sustain`). Swept in the claim's own scope — a lead **not faster than the ego and not accelerating** (`v_lead <= v_ego`, `a_lead <= 0`) — the aged candidate is never shallower on `TestStalenessScope`'s grid at 46 ms, at 0.176 s (that plus the ~0.13 s learned radar latency `_lead_age` adds, the age a radar lead is actually planned at) and at 0.5 s; decaying over the age as well read up to +0.056 m/s² shallower at 0.176 s. Outside the claim's scope (a lead faster than the ego, or accelerating) both arms differ in both directions and always have. The closed-loop stop stays essentially the fresh one as staleness grows (stopping at a stationary lead from 25 m/s: 5.2583 m fresh, 5.2557 m at the measured 46 ms, 5.2179 m at 0.5 s, against 4.6356 m uncorrected at 0.5 s). The alternative — folding a sensor-latency term into `action_t` — was measured and is wrong, because `action_t` projects the ego too: it made the same case *worse* (0.17 m of margin lost uncorrected, 0.28 m with the uniform bump), which is why this ages one stream rather than advancing both. A missing or equal stamp gives the zero correction, which is what keeps the maneuver plant's bare dict (it has no `logMonoTime` at all) and upstream's harness behaving exactly as before. It is also the one piece of this fork validated end to end rather than in simulation: replaying real logged messages through the planner on their own `logMonoTime` gives the same ages the sweep predicted — 40.4, 40.6, 45.2 and 46.2 ms median, max 146.6 ms, across the four segments the corpus has with engaged longitudinal control, a lead present on 500–1200 of each segment's ~1200 ticks (re-run today, `python -m moonpilot.lab lead-ages`: median 35.8 ms on `0000043f` and 47.4 ms on `0000043e`, max 48.0 and 50.6 — the two newest engaged drives, whose committed planner code is HEAD's minus two engine-coast-only commits; the four-segment corpus is not in this checkout) — and the correction moves the command by at most 0.175 m/s² there, with no NaN and nothing out of the actuator's range over 4797 real ticks. The other half of the chain stays uncompensated on purpose, and that is measured rather than assumed: the whole 255-segment corpus contains only 4 segments with any engaged longitudinal control (13,207 of 1.14 M `carControl` messages, one car, `TOYOTA_RAV4_TSS2`), and its command is quasi-static — median |Δaccel| is 0.000 per 10 ms sample and only 2.5–5.8 % of engaged samples move more than 0.02 m/s² — so jerk-domain, event-pooled and first-order-plus-delay estimators all peak at the noise floor with best lags scattering past causality (−310 ms). Amplitude exists, bandwidth does not. `CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay` = 0.15 s is therefore comma's own calibrated default, not a fork finding, and a corpus that could test it needs sustained *changing* long-control, not merely more engaged minutes.
- **Delay compensation is state prediction, not plan inversion.** The policy is evaluated where the car will be at `longitudinalActuatorDelay + DT_MDL` (`action_t`) and each lead at that plus its own age — `lead_age`, the bullet above — instead of inverting the published plan through `get_accel_from_plan`. That inverse divides by the delay, so a step in the plan comes out amplified by `action_t / dt` — measured at 45 m/s³ and instant `ACCEL_MIN` saturation in simulation, which is why this is the fork's mechanism.
- **The actuator delay it projects through is measured, not assumed.** `action_t` is `max(CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay, the live estimate) + DT_MDL`. The estimate comes from `moonpilot/latency.py` — `lagd`'s machinery (a masked normalized cross-correlation over a 90 s window, then a block average) driven by this chain's own signal pair, the planner's published `a_target` against `carState.aEgo`, with its own ROI and gates: 0.05–0.60 s, a 0.5 m/s² excitation floor on *both* signals, ≥25 s of valid samples in the window, and a 2 s hold-off marking the frames after any invalid one. It is fed only where the fork planner's command is what moves the car — engaged, in `pid`, above 5 m/s, and not at a standstill, on a pedal or under `forceDecel` — so an unengaged stretch or a driver override teaches it nothing. `CP.longitudinalActuatorDelay` is upstream's `0.15` for every car without an override, marked `# TODO estimate car specific lag` (`opendbc/car/interfaces.py`), so this is the only thing that can move it on a car like the 2021 RAV4 XLE; the lag for the *lateral* chain is already measured live by `moonpilot/lagd.py` (upstream's `lagd` with a fork memory) and read by both torque controllers, which is why this is longitudinal-only. **The live value may only lengthen the projection**: the maneuver suite and the stopping floor's bounds were validated at or above the stock constant, and the estimator's own failure modes bias it low — a command that never moves, a long steady ramp (measured: a linear ramp reports the ROI's *floor*, 0.05 s, at `corr` 1.0), all of which `max` makes inert. What it takes to reach a usable estimate is a full window plus 5 blocks of 5 s (25 s) — and, on a car whose engagement comes in short stretches, more than one drive. The block count rides with the value in `MoonpilotLongLagBlocks` (`INT`), so a drive that earns a block or two hands them on instead of restarting; the carried mean is applied only once the blocks are there, and both params are read at construction (a value written before the count existed was only ever written when trusted, so a missing count still seeds as trusted). - **`MoonpilotCurveSpeed` is three bounded braking terms on the model's *predicted* path, all inside the cruise slot.** A kinematic pre-brake against the curve's own distance — the shape the lead stopping floor had before it moved to the relative frame, `(v_target² - v_ego²) / 2d` — which is right here, since a curve does not drive away over every admitted sample, the deepest winning — a jerk-limited target speed, and a proportional in-curve regulator on the lateral accel the car is *actually* pulling. - **The jerk ceiling is what makes a tightening entry slow earlier rather than harder.** Lateral jerk at constant speed is `v³·|dκ/ds|`, so a ceiling on jerk *is* a ceiling on speed — `cbrt(J_LAT / |dκ/ds|)` — and that target falls as the curvature ramps in, which is the one thing a measured lateral-accel limit cannot see. `|dκ/ds|` is a centered difference over `MOONPILOT_CURVE_JERK_STEP` = 2 m, clamped to the path's own ends and divided by the span it actually used, rather than between the model's own samples — whose spacing runs 0.1 m to 12 m along one path, so a sample-scale gradient's value depends on where it happens to sit, and the pre-brake takes the *deepest* sample of the window, so that choice goes to whichever sample is noisiest. Over 143,058 corpus frames, the frames where only the ceiling asks for braking (741, 0.52 %) split by exactly that: the 33 % a 2 m window drops sit at a median 0.47 m spacing with incoherent steps (net over summed |dκ| 0.42, |κ| at 0.6× its own ±5 m neighbourhood) — a single sample's step, which no road has and the model's own 0.1 s sampling cannot have resolved — while the 67 % it keeps are coherent ramps at 1.7 m spacing with |κ| at 1.7× their neighbourhood, at full depth. A 5 m window was measured too and dropped coherent ramps (the dropped set's coherence median 1.0), so the window is sized to the sub-meter class rather than to the ramp the term exists for, and nowhere in the corpus does it ask for *more* braking than the sample scale did (0 of 143,058 frames). The window is a **scale**, not a coherence filter, and that is its named ceiling: it attenuates structure narrower than itself by roughly the ratio of the two, so at the model's wider highway spacing a sub-meter step is only halved and can still bind — the corpus that measured this runs 8–18 m/s, where the same step sits at 0.47 m and is dropped outright. A guard on the binding sample's own coherence (κ at 1.7× its ±5 m neighbourhood versus 0.6× for the two classes) is what would separate them at every spacing, and it is machinery for its own measured change rather than this one. A path with `dκ/ds = 1e-4` 1/m² caps the entry at `cbrt(3.0/1e-4)` ≈ 31.1 m/s — above the 21.79 m/s a 250 m radius is worth, so on a 40 m ramp the budget governs and the ceiling is inert — while a ramp tight enough to reach `dκ/ds = 4e-4` (10 m) puts the ceiling at 19.6 m/s and it is the ceiling that governs: at 25 m/s braking then begins 93.0 m out against 90.0 m with `MOONPILOT_CURVE_J_LAT` raised to 100, which is the whole of what the term buys. A constant-radius path has no gradient at all and is bounded by the budget alone.

- **The bank is credited exactly the way `clip_curvature` credits it.** The achievable lateral-accel band is shifted by `g·roll` — `lat_accel_budget`'s one formula, used by every term, numpy-broadcast over the per-sample turn directions — so a banked left-hander is worth more and a right-hander less by the same amount, and a roll large enough to drive the budget under `MOONPILOT_CURVE_A_LAT_MIN` floors there rather than asking for a stop. - **The learned lateral scale is one-sided, and that is the whole safety argument.** `LatAccelBiasEstimator` pairs `|orientationRate.z|·velocity.x` from the model's path — what the plan predicted it would pull — with the measured `curvature·v_ego²` one second later, at `vehicleParameters.valid` car speeds, and only while lateral control is ours (engaged, not `steeringPressed`, moving, not at a standstill); a straight-line frame teaches nothing because both magnitudes must clear `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_MIN_LAT_ACCEL`, and an invalid frame empties the pairing queue so a prediction never scores a measurement from the other side of a disengage. `applied()` is clamped at 1.0 and bounded at `MOONPILOT_CURVE_BIAS_MAX`: a car realizing *more* lateral accel than the path predicted is effectively in a tighter curve and should slow more, while the opposite direction would hand the feature *less* braking than the geometry says, which is the one direction this cannot be allowed to fail in. Stopped or absent leads — the cases the upstream predicate was written for — are untouched, and so is resume: a lead that pulls away lifts the command over the 0.1 threshold by itself, and both `long_control_state_trans`'s cruise-standstill pin and controlsd's resume read this flag. How long that takes is a property of the lead, not a fixed frame count: the release threshold is `0.3 * dgap + 0.6 * v_lead >= 0.1`, i.e. `dgap + 2 * v_lead >= 0.333 m`, so any lead above ~0.17 m/s clears it on speed alone and the car moves within a frame, while a slower creep has to open that third of a meter of gap first — measured 1.55 s at 0.1 m/s, 5.35 s at 0.05, 16.6 s at 0.02 and 123 s at 0.003, the car sitting still for as long as it takes the lead to get ~0.33 m away, which is the intended reading of "the lead has barely moved". Below ~0.1 m/s of lead speed the gate's own deadband leaves the flag chattering on the 0.1 boundary (measured 20–40 flips in 20 s, pre-existing), i.e. the car inches rather than creeps; that band is the deadband's edge and not a stall. A lead decelerating to rest crosses the deadband and the upstream predicate takes over, so when a genuine stop engages is unchanged and every upstream maneuver combination still passes. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins the creep closed-loop, the two narrowed-out cases, and that stopped-lead and no-lead still declare the stop.
- **The deadband the stop gate keys on is 12σ of the lead-speed noise at rest, and that was measured rather than assumed.** `MOONPILOT_SHOULD_STOP_SPEED` sits on raw `radarState` `vLead` with no filter behind it, unlike `leadd`'s probability and in-path channels, which reads as a bare threshold on a noisy signal. It is not: over 371 s of corpus where the ego is parked and the lead is provably stationary (`dRel` moving under 5 cm across ±0.5 s, 7,423 frames, 40 segments), `vLead` has sd 0.0084 m/s, max +0.05, and **zero** frames above the 0.1 gate — the channel is quantized at 0.025 m/s and effectively silent at rest, where the moving case is noisier — 0.024–0.035 m/s of `vLead` noise measured across speed bands on the 199-segment corpus. One 2-frame excursion to +0.327 exists in the whole `v_ego < 0.3` band, and a radard-style 0.2 s filter would only take it to +0.114, so filtering buys nothing here. What the corpus cannot speak for is the creep band itself: it holds 2 frames of the ego moving between 0.1 and 0.3 m/s behind a stationary lead, so the gate's behavior there rests on the closed-loop tests rather than on logs.
- **Nothing holds a stop once the lead leaves the slot, and that is upstream's behavior, measured rather than inherited by accident.** With a live set speed and no lead in `radarState`, the cruise candidate is the only one left and it asks to go: flown from rest behind a stopped lead at the standstill gap whose slot then vanishes, the fork commands +1.57 m/s², crosses 0.1 m/s 0.25 s later and reaches the lead's former position in 2.95 s. Upstream's planner in the identical scenario is *faster* — it steps straight to +1.60 on the first frame with no launch ramp and crosses 0.1 m/s at 0.10 s — so a fork-owned guard here would be a divergence, not a fix, and neither planner has any memory of the lead it was holding for. It is also not a case the logs produce: over 44 real stops in the corpus (ego above 5 m/s with a radar-confirmed lead inside 50 m, decelerating to rest, the lead still present at the moment of rest, 556 s stopped) the slot is empty for **2.10 s in total, 0.378 % of stopped time**, in 2 of the 44 stops, worst single gap 1.75 s — and both of those stops are at 30.8 m and 11.7 m of gap, while all 42 stops with a close lead (3–6 m, the geometry where pulling away would hit something) hold `present` at 1.000 for the entire standstill. The flicker that does exist is in *parked* frames rather than stops behind a car (lead present on only 47.6 % of them, 98 dropouts, median 0.55 s), which is clutter coming and going rather than a car being lost. Bridging it on the model would not work either: `leadsV3[0].prob` during those gaps peaks at 0.46–0.499, just under the 0.5 radard itself refuses a vision lead at. If a close-lead dropout ever does show up in logs, the shape to reach for is a short hold of the last governing lead across the gap, not a change to the stop predicate. This 14-segment route agrees and returns no-go on the hold: 145 dropout runs, 140 re-acquired at median 0.41 s; 34 moving-close dropouts, 32 of them track-id swaps to a different object; zero dropouts releasing braking the geometry still calls for; every stop behind a car holding `present` (266/266 and 110/110 at 4.8–5.3 m). Acquisition churn at 40–64 m with the command essentially unmoved was not a reason to add state there; route `000003f8` is the case where far churn did move the command, and the hold below is scoped to it.
- **A far radar lead outlives a lapse in radard's association — and only a far one.** Toyota's radar and the model disagree about a far lead: on route `000003f8` the model's range ran ~14 m short (median) and its speed ±6–7 m/s off (p10/p90), so past ~60 m radard kept failing to match and handed `leadOne` to the vision-only lead (84 % of vision-only leads on that route were past 60 m, median 93 m) or to nothing for 0.25–1.5 s. On segment 42 that alternated a 14 m/s radar track with a 21 m/s vision lead 20 m nearer, and the arrival command stepped −0.5 ↔ +0.1 seven times in four seconds. `_radar_hold` keeps the last radar `leadOne`, projected forward with `lead_state_at`, as an extra `lead0` candidate for `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_T` (2 s) after it stops being the slot; the current lead stays a candidate beside it, so `min` still brakes for a stopped car only vision sees. It is released at once below `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_MIN_V` (5 m/s) or inside `MOONPILOT_RADAR_HOLD_MIN_GAP` (40 m), so the bullet above still holds: nothing holds a stop, or a close cut-out. Its `modelProb` is zero, so FCW never fires on a held track.
- **One vehicle is one lead, whichever radar tracks it arrives on.** radard tracks a car as several returns and can match both model leads to the nearest one, so near a stop a single stopped car filled `leadOne` and `leadTwo` on two tracks whose speeds disagreed by a few tenths (route `00000400` segment 4, tracks 517/541, swapping every 0.1–0.3 s). Each swap refitted the lead accel from three samples or fell back to the new track's `aLeadK` (±0.6 m/s² on a car at rest), and `min` over the two readings handed the win back and forth: the command pulsed −0.88 → −1.26 → −0.75 m/s² at 1.9 m/s, and the car followed. `moonpilot.lead.same_car` (within 1 m of range, 1 m of lateral offset and 1 m/s of speed, all at once) now keeps `LeadAccelEstimator`'s window across a track change on the same car, and the planner drops `leadTwo` from the plan's candidates when it is the same car as `leadOne`. The lateral bound is what keeps a car alongside — same range, same speed, a lane over, and possibly braking differently — two leads: same-car track pairs on hand sat at median 0.1 m and p99 0.8 m of `yRel` apart, against a lane's ~3.5 m, and anything ambiguous stays two candidates. FCW still reads both slots: the two matches can carry different `modelProb`, and the dropped one may be the only one over FCW's 0.9 bar. Replayed, that stop's last 4.5 s of command jitter went 0.090 → 0.056 m/s² and the pulse to −0.84 → −1.04 → −0.89; what remains is the one track's own `vLead` noise against the model's candidate, which `min` still arbitrates.
- **Throttle is lost under the model's 0.4 gas-press probability and returns only over 0.5, held for `MOONPILOT_ALLOW_THROTTLE_RETURN_T`.** Upstream's gate (`allowThrottle`, the coast cap in `cruise_cap`) has no hysteresis, and upstream's MPC smooths what this planner passes straight through: on segment 43 the probability hovered on 0.4 behind a slowing lead, a third of the no-throttle runs lasted one frame (median three), and the command dithered −0.2 ↔ −0.42. The signal is good — in that route's manual driving the driver was on the gas on 3 % of frames under 0.4 and 78 % over it — so `MOONPILOT_ALLOW_THROTTLE_RELEASE` adds hysteresis rather than moving the threshold: engaged no-throttle runs went 27 → 18, one-frame runs 9 → 1. The 0.1 band is narrower than the signal's own frame-to-frame swing, though: on route `0000040e` it read 0.31 → 0.53 → 0.38 → 0.66 inside 0.2 s and the cruise term stepped +0.08 ↔ −0.30 three times in one second ahead of a stop (338.4–339.3 s). Throttle now returns only after the probability has held over 0.5 for 0.5 s, and losing it stays immediate, so a coast starts as early as before and holds. Replayed over the cached engaged segments, the flip-flops on `00000400` (+0.45 ↔ −0.14) and `00000401` (+0.09 ↔ −0.21) become steady coasts (re-run today, `python -m moonpilot.lab curve-metrics` counts the permission bit's changes on the current planner: 5 flips at 0.054/s on `00000400` and 4 at 0.078/s on `00000401`). The tradeoff is longer coasting near the band: `00000401` holds a coast for roughly 9 s toward a slower lead. That replay follows the logged ego trajectory; it does not measure the changed closed-loop speed or gap.

The acceleration controller keeps upstream's PI/feedforward family and the car's own `longitudinalTuning.kiV` schedule. `long_control_state_trans` remains upstream's contract. In `stopping`, a physically rolling car rate-limits toward `min(a_target, -MOONPILOT_MIN_ROLLING_STOP_DECEL)` (−0.2 m/s² minimum): stronger braking stays reachable and zero or slightly positive stop targets still bring the wheels to rest. Only `CS.standstill`, not the PCM's cruise-standstill bit, starts the `MOONPILOT_STOPPING_JERK` ramp toward `CP.stopAccel`. Acceleration limits apply in either branch. A `stopping → pid` edge retains its negative braking baseline; only a positive plan resets that baseline to zero so the brake hold releases promptly. This removes the repeated brake-release pulse on negative-plan state chatter without adding another stop state machine. At the reproduced signal-stop state, a plan of −0.33 m/s² remains −0.33 while rolling instead of ramping to −1.23. (`python -m moonpilot.lab longcontrol` replays this controller at its own 100 Hz on the logged plan and the logged `carState`, and reproduces the logged `carControl.actuators.accel` to 0.0000 m/s² p95 over 41,879 engaged frames of `0000043f` — the controller chain is exact there — and to 0.0250 m/s² when it is fed the replayed plan instead, which adds the planner's own 0.0032; feeding it planner-rate ticks instead of control ticks, or leaving `standstill` off the fake `carState`, is the difference between that and a wrong controller, so the replay refuses a feed that is not at `DT_CTRL`.) Toyota's downstream PID, permission hysteresis and standstill release are unchanged. Logged-trajectory replay and synthetic plants cannot prove physical brake release or cabin comfort; those require an on-device drive.

One trap is worth naming: `log.LongitudinalPersonality.standard` is a plain `int`, but the same enum read off a message is a capnp `_DynamicEnum` whose hash is not that int, so a dict keyed by the enum's members never matches a value read off `selfdriveState` — `MOONPILOT_T_FOLLOW` is keyed by the raw value for that reason. `moonpilot/tests/test_longitudinal.py` pins it, along with the properties above, and ends by running upstream's own maneuver suite — all 12 maneuvers (11 under `forceDecel`) from `openpilot/selfdrive/test/longitudinal_maneuvers/test_longitudinal.py`, with `MoonpilotExperimentalMode` pinned on and off, patched onto the plant — against the fork planner, which is the end-to-end statement that the strategy drives the stock scenarios without a crash, without stalling at a stop, and while still decelerating under `forceDecel`. Upstream's plant no longer builds `carControl` or `vehicleParameters`, so the harness supplies their defaults. `moonpilot/tests/test_longcontrol.py` holds the controller to controlsd's call contract and to the three things the fork owns in it.

`process_replay` reference logs differ for plannerd and controlsd with the feature on, and for selfdrived on every fork build regardless of any param — `subs=["selfdriveState", "onroadEvents"]` with only `logMonoTime` ignored, so the startup alert the banner seam swaps in is in the recorded frames too.
